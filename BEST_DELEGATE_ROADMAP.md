### WHAT EACH PHASE INCLUDES

#### PHASE 1: RESEARCH [Where 70% of delegates fail]
We will NOT do generic research. For YOUR country + agenda, I will deliver:

- **Country Position:** Official stance, foreign ministry statements, UN speeches (last 2 years)
- **Treaties/Resolutions/Conventions:** Exact numbers and years you can quote (e.g., "India voted against UNSC 2728 because...")
- **Voting Pattern:** How your country actually voted, not what it should have
- **Hard Stats:** Numbers you can drop in speeches - budget, ranking, casualties, trade, military spend
- **Other Blocs:** Who agrees with you, who hates you, who is on fence
- **Your Weaknesses:** The 3 things opponents WILL attack you on - and how to pre-empt them
- **Recent Developments:** Last 6-12 months - this is where Best Delegates win

**Deliverable in repo:** `prep/research/01_country_brief.md` + `02_bloc_analysis.md`

#### PHASE 2: CORE POLICY
- Your 3-5 non-negotiable arguments (The Spine)
- What you can compromise on vs. what you never compromise on
- 5-6 resolution clauses written in YOUR country's voice
- Diplomatic phrasing to defend the indefensible

#### PHASE 3: SPEECHES [Indian MUN Style]
Forget "Honourable Chair, Esteemed Delegates..." for 20 seconds.

Indian chairs want:
- **First 15 sec:** Hook + country + stance (clear)
- **Middle 30 sec:** ONE argument + ONE fact/stat/treaty
- **Last 15 sec:** Solution or challenge

You will get:
- Opening speech (90 sec) - memorable, not cringe
- GSL (60 sec) - adaptable
- 6-8 Mod speeches (30-45 sec each) for most likely subtopics
- 20-30 sec interventions
- Closing speech (60 sec)
- All speeches will have: opening line, data point, closing line that forces a POI or applause

**Deliverable:** `prep/speeches/`

#### PHASE 4: POI BANK
For each major opponent (we identify 5-7):
- Question you ask
- Weakness it targets
- What they will likely answer
- Your follow-up that traps them
- How to use their answer in your next speech

And for YOU:
- 10 toughest questions you'll face
- For each: Safe answer / Strong answer / 1-line comeback

**Deliverable:** `prep/poi/bank.md`

#### PHASE 5 & 6: MODS + UNMODS [Where Best Delegate is Decided]
- 5 motions YOU should propose (wording + why it helps YOU)
- Who to include, who to isolate
- Unmod: First 5 people to approach, exact opening line, what to offer each, how to become bloc leader without being called "dominating"
- Chit strategy: What to write to chair (Indian MUNs love chits)

#### PHASE 7 & 8: RESOLUTION + SIMULATION
- We write operative clauses together
- Then we FIGHT. I will be the delegate who interrupts you, the chair who asks a nasty POI, the ally who betrays you. After each of your answers, you get:
  1. Score /10
  2. What was weak (Content / Diplomacy / Accuracy / Persuasion / Delivery / Strategy)
  3. Rewritten version that would survive against a strong delegate

## HOW INDIAN SCHOOL/COLLEGE MUNS ARE ACTUALLY JUDGED

Chairs will never tell you this directly:

| Average Delegate | Good Delegate | Best Delegate Contender |
|------------------|---------------|-------------------------|
| Speaks only when given chance, reads from paper | Speaks often, tries to make points, but repeats same argument | Speaks strategically - first 30 mins establishes, middle leads, final defends. Every speech adds NEW value |
| Research = BG + Wikipedia | Research = BG + some treaties | Research = Treaties + voting record + recent MEA statements + stats no one else has. Can quote source & year |
| POIs are generic: "What does delegate think about human rights?" | POIs are relevant but safe | POIs expose contradiction between country's vote and statement. Follow-up kills. |
| In unmods, sits with friends, waits to be invited | Joins a bloc, contributes an idea | CREATES or LEADS a bloc, writes 2-3 operative clauses, gets sponsors, merges blocs, knows when to compromise |
| Resolution: Signs whatever is available | Sponsors resolution, has one clause | Owns 2-3 key clauses, can defend every clause, attacks other's clauses with evidence |
| Chits: "Great speech!" | Chits with questions | Chits that show research + help chair remember your name |

**Brutal truth:** Speaking fluently is 30%. The other 70% is research depth, bloc control, and consistency. A fluent speaker with weak research NEVER gets Best Delegate in a good IIT Delhi committee. A less fluent but deeply researched, strategically dominant delegate often does.

## STRATEGY PREVIEW: HOW YOU WIN

**BEFORE COMMITTEE:** Research > Position > Allies mapped > Speeches ready > POIs memorized

**FIRST 30 MINUTES:**
- Do NOT try to speak first if you are not ready. Speak 3rd-5th, but make it count.
- First speech = Stance + 1 powerful fact + 1 solution. No story.
- Send 2 chits in first hour: One to chair (subtle research flex), one to potential ally ("USA - align on clause 3? - India")
- Identify: Who is prepared? Who is loud but empty? Who is your direct opponent?

**MIDDLE OF COMMITTEE:**
- Raise ONE mod caucus topic that benefits YOUR bloc. This is how you become relevant.
- In every mod speech, reference previous delegate's point and either support or contradict with evidence. Shows listening.
- Volunteer to be in drafting group. Say: "India would like to contribute language on operative clause 4, we have precedent from..."

**UNMODS:**
- Don't wait. Stand up first. Go to your pre-identified ally #1 and #2.
- Script: "Hey, US - I noticed we both want X, but China is pushing Y. If we combine our clauses on X, we can get numbers. I have draft language, want to see?"
- Never say "Join my bloc." Say "Let's build this together, I like your point on..."
- To become leader: Be the one with the laptop/doc, be the one who writes down compromises, be the one who presents to chair.

**FINAL PHASE:**
- Defend YOUR clauses with treaty/stat. Attack opponent's clause by showing it contradicts their own policy.
- Closing speech: Not summary. Legacy. "This committee will be judged not by how many resolutions we passed, but whether we..."

**HOW TO STAND OUT WITHOUT BEING ANNOYING:**
- Speak 30% less than the loudest delegate, but make every speech 3x denser.
- Never repeat a point someone else made without adding new evidence.
- Yield to POIs selectively. If strong, take them. If hostile + weak, yield to chair.
- Chits > Speaking sometimes. A well-timed chit to chair with a fact correction is remembered.
