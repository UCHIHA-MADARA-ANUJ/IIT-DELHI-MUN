# 00 — GSL APPROACH + TARGETING STRATEGY

> This is the strategy file for Brazil's GSL phase.
>
> **Do not write the final speech before deciding what the speech is trying to achieve.**

---

# 1. THE REAL OBJECTIVE OF THE GSL

The GSL is NOT mainly for:

- explaining the entire agenda;
- proving that Brazil researched more than everyone;
- listing every Brazilian proposal;
- attacking every country;
- showing every statistic we know.

The GSL should do four jobs:

1. **Establish Brazil's identity.**
2. **Put one clear idea into the room.**
3. **Make delegates interested in talking to Brazil.**
4. **Create the first coalition around Brazil's language.**

The best GSL leaves people thinking:

> **“Brazil has a clear position and something concrete we can negotiate around.”**

---

# 2. OUR BASIC SPEAKING POSITION

Brazil's speaking spine:

**PROHIBIT → REGULATE → CONTROL → REVIEW → ACCOUNT**

Meaning:

- **PROHIBIT** clearly unacceptable autonomous systems/uses.
- **REGULATE** systems that remain permissible.
- **CONTROL** preserve effective human judgement/control.
- **REVIEW** continue legal and technical assurance across the lifecycle.
- **ACCOUNT** keep responsibility and evidence traceable.

This is the thread running through the entire GSL phase.

---

# 3. WHAT WE WANT THE ROOM TO BELIEVE ABOUT BRAZIL

After the first few speeches, we want delegates to see Brazil as:

### A. A serious legal delegate
Brazil understands that IHL already applies.

### B. A practical delegate
Brazil is not satisfied with “international cooperation”.

### C. A technical bridge
Brazil can talk to both treaty-oriented and cautious/technical States.

### D. A drafting delegate
Brazil has actual mechanisms that can become clauses.

### E. A cross-regional delegate
Brazil is not presenting a purely European, purely Global South, or purely technological view.

---

# 4. WHAT WE DO NOT WANT TO LOOK LIKE

Avoid becoming:

### THE “BAN EVERYTHING” DELEGATE
We are proposing a two-tier structure, not a blanket ban on every autonomous military capability.

### THE “AI IS EVIL” DELEGATE
Talk about functions, risks and safeguards.

### THE “IHL IS USELESS” DELEGATE
IHL is the foundation.

### THE “UNGA VOTE = EVERYONE AGREES WITH BRAZIL” DELEGATE
Votes show political engagement, not identical treaty positions.

### THE “STATISTICS MACHINE”
One strong number beats seven random numbers.

### THE “ONE-SPEECH DELEGATE”
Do not repeat the same 90 seconds in every MOD.

---

# 5. THE FIRST GSL STRATEGY

The first GSL should establish:

**PROBLEM → IHL → BRAZIL'S TWO-TIER POSITION → ONE DISTINCTIVE MECHANISM → OPENING FOR COLLABORATION**

The distinctive mechanism should preferably be:

### OPTION A — OPERATING ENVELOPE
Most understandable.

### OPTION B — MATERIAL-CHANGE REVIEW
More original.

### OPTION C — ASSURANCE CASE
Most distinctive.

For the first speech, do not explain all three in detail.

---

# 6. THE FIRST GSL SHOULD NOT BE A COUNTRY ATTACK

Do NOT make the opening speech:

> “The United States is wrong...”
>
> “Russia is blocking...”
>
> “Japan must...”

That immediately creates unnecessary resistance.

Instead:

> “Brazil believes…”

Then make the room choose whether to respond to the substance.

Country-specific pressure can come later through POIs, MODs and interventions.

## TARGET GROUP B — BRIDGE STATES

These delegates may not accept Brazil's full legal-form position, but they can support practical safeguards.

Current examples:

- Japan
- Australia
- India
- Canada
- China
- Republic of Korea
- United Kingdom

The goal is NOT:

> “Accept Brazil's whole treaty package.”

The goal is:

> **“Which concrete safeguards can you support?”**

If they contribute to a clause, they become part of the drafting conversation.

---

## TARGET GROUP C — PROCEDURAL EXPANSION

These are States with current procedural convergence around using the GGE material as a basis for further negotiations.

The repository identifies 25 committee delegations in the current 47-State procedural intersection.

The first question is:

> **“Which part of the GGE material do you want to move forward?”**

Then move toward clause-level cooperation.

---

# 8. WHO WE SHOULD NOT WASTE THE FIRST GSL ON

Do not build the opening speech around delegates whose current positions are very difficult to move on legal form.

That does not mean ignoring them.

It means:

**GSL = room-building**

not

**GSL = full confrontation**

We can challenge hard positions later when the room understands Brazil's architecture.

---

# 9. THE “TARGET” DOES NOT MEAN “ATTACK”

For this MUN, targeting means:

> **choosing who should hear and respond to each argument.**

Example:

### Human-control speech
Target:
- technical bridge states;
- treaty-oriented States concerned with human judgement.

### Capacity-building speech
Target:
- Global South;
- African;
- Asian;
- smaller delegations.

### Legal-form speech
Target:
- binding-instrument supporters;
- procedural delegates who are already willing to move toward negotiations.

### Verification speech
Target:
- technical/security-oriented delegates.

This lets us build a wider coalition without using the same argument on everyone.

# 11. JAPAN TARGETING PLAN

### Goal

Do not force Japan to announce:

> “We support Brazil's binding treaty.”

Instead get:

1. bloc participation;
2. one technical drafting contribution;
3. one shared clause;
4. continued conversation.

### First message

> “Hey! We’re forming a bloc for DISEC and wanted to ask if Japan would be interested in joining us. We’re working on the LAWS agenda and would be really happy to have Japan with us.”

### Follow-up

> “We’re mainly working around clear restrictions on unacceptable autonomy, strong human judgement/control, testing and lifecycle safeguards. Which part would Japan be most interested in working on?”

### Desired outcome

Japan becomes a contributor on:

- definition/characterization;
- predictability;
- testing;
- operating limits;
- defensive systems;
- human involvement.

# 14. THE GSL SHOULD BUILD THE BLOC INDIRECTLY

Do not say every five minutes:

> “Join Brazil.”

Instead make speeches that cause delegates to think:

> “I agree with that part.”

Then lobbying starts.

Useful lines:

> “We welcome States that want to turn these safeguards into concrete language.”

> “Brazil is ready to work with delegations on the operational details.”

> “The wording can be negotiated; the safeguards must remain real.”

---

# 15. SPEECH → LOBBYING PIPELINE

Every GSL should create a follow-up conversation.

Example:

### Speech

> “Brazil proposes operating envelopes.”

### Lobbying

> “Would your delegation support target, geographic and temporal limits?”

### Drafting

> “Can you help us write that clause?”

That is the entire objective.

---

# 16. DO WE “CALL OUT” OTHER STATES?

Yes, but selectively.

### EARLY COMMITTEE
Prefer indirect pressure.

> “Some delegations argue existing law alone is enough. Brazil asks: how do we demonstrate compliance when the system changes?”

This creates a challenge without naming anyone.

### MID COMMITTEE
Name a position when responding to a specific argument.

### LATE COMMITTEE
Use sharper rebuttals if another delegation directly attacks Brazil's architecture.

---

# 17. WHO WE SHOULD NOT NAME IN THE FIRST GSL

Avoid naming:

- USA;
- Russia;
- China;
- India;
- Japan;
- Australia

in the opening GSL unless specifically necessary.

Their positions are too useful as future diplomatic openings to spend immediately.

The first GSL should sell Brazil's framework.

> “Brazil proposes an operating envelope, assurance case, procurement gate, protected review panel, assurance passport, regional testing hub...”

> **One speech = one mechanism.**

We need one idea that delegates associate with Brazil.

Recommended candidate:

> **“Operating Envelope.”**

Why:

- simple;
- technical;
- easy to explain;
- easy to draft;

- works with bridge States;

# 28. WHAT COUNTS AS A SUCCESSFUL “TARGET”

A target is successful if the delegate:

### LEVEL 1
Listens.

### LEVEL 2
Agrees with one principle.

### LEVEL 3
Agrees to discuss a clause.

### LEVEL 4
Contributes wording.

### LEVEL 5
Defends the wording in the room.

The actual goal is Level 4–5.

---

# 29. THE “DO NOT CHASE” RULE

If a delegate:

- repeatedly refuses every mechanism;
- refuses all discussion;
- says they will not join anything;
- gives no substantive opening;

do not waste five minutes.

Leave with:

> “No problem. If any of our safeguards overlap with your position later, come find us.”

Then move on.

---

# 30. THE “HIGH-VALUE CONVERSATION” TEST

After talking to someone, ask:

> Did I learn what they support?

> Did we identify one shared clause?

> Did they agree to help draft?

If all three are no:

**low-value conversation.**

If one is yes:

**keep contact.**

If two or three are yes:

**high-value target.**
