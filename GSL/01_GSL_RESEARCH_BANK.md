# 1. CURRENT 2026 DIPLOMATIC CONTEXT

## 2026 GGE

The GGE met:

- 2–6 March 2026
- 31 August–4 September 2026

Its mandate was to formulate by consensus elements of an instrument **without prejudging its legal nature**, alongside other possible measures.

### Why it matters for Brazil

The debate has moved into substantive negotiating material.

### Safe speech line

> “States are no longer starting from zero; the 2026 GGE has worked through substantive elements for a future instrument.”

### Do not say

> “The GGE agreed on a treaty.”

---

# 2. 2026 REVIEW-CONFERENCE MOMENT

The Seventh CCW Review Conference is scheduled for **16–20 November 2026**.

### Why it matters

The committee is discussing LAWS during an active institutional transition.

### Safe speech line

> “The next institutional step should build on the substantive work already completed rather than restart the debate from zero.”

---

# 3. CURRENT GGE SCOPE DISCUSSION

The 2026 Chair's summary worked with a characterization centered on a functionally integrated weapon/technology combination capable of identifying, selecting and engaging a target without human intervention in execution.

The discussion also raised:

- what “identify” means;
- prior human programming;
- fire-and-forget / homing systems;
- minimal human validation;
- scope loopholes.

### Brazil speech use

> “Definitions should follow autonomous functions, not platform labels.”

---

# 4. IHL — THE FOUNDATION

For GSL purposes, know:

- distinction;
- proportionality;
- precautions;
- applicable restrictions on means and methods of warfare;
- continuing human responsibility.

### Best Brazil formulation

> “Existing IHL remains the legal foundation; Brazil's proposal is to make compliance operational in the autonomous-weapons context.”

---

# 5. ARTICLE 36

Article 36 of Additional Protocol I is a legal-review obligation for States Parties to that Protocol.

### Brazil use

> “For States to which Article 36 applies, weapons review is already a key legal obligation; Brazil supports strengthening continuing review for autonomous systems more broadly.”

### Never say

> “Article 36 binds every State.”

---

# 6. HUMAN JUDGEMENT / CONTROL

The exact phrase “meaningful human control” is not a universally codified standalone treaty rule.

The useful speech concept is:

- understand;
- assess;
- constrain;
- authorize;
- monitor;
- intervene where feasible;
- reassess;
- account.

### Speech line

> “Human presence is not the same as effective human judgement.”

---

# 7. ACCURACY VS PREDICTABILITY

### Accuracy

Performance on a task.

### Predictability

Ability to anticipate behavior under relevant operating conditions.

### Speech line

> “A system can be accurate on average and still be unpredictable outside the conditions in which it was validated.”

Connect to:

**Operating Envelope + Testing.**

---

# 8. RELIABILITY VS PREDICTABILITY

Reliability is consistent performance against a defined requirement.

Predictability concerns whether users can anticipate behavior across relevant conditions.

### Speech line

> “A system may be reliable inside a narrow environment and unsafe outside it.”

---

# 9. ADAPTIVE AI

Main question:

> “Does the original assurance still describe the system after it changes?”

Potential material changes:

- target classifier;
- engagement logic;
- autonomy mode;
- sensor fusion;
- model update;
- target category;
- operating environment.

### Brazil mechanism

**Material-Change Review**

---

# 10. TESTING

Do not say only:

> “The system should be tested.”

Know the testing categories:

- normal;
- edge;
- degraded;
- hostile;
- civilian-complex;
- interaction;
- failure;
- change.

### Speech line

> “Test the conditions that can break the system's assumptions.”

---

# 11. OPERATING ENVELOPE

A proposed Brazil/AAWG mechanism defining:

- target;
- geography;
- duration;
- scale;
- environment;
- communications assumptions;
- intervention/deactivation conditions.

### Speech line

> “Human control becomes more concrete when the system's authority is bounded by an operating envelope.”

---

# 12. ACCOUNTABILITY

State should be able to reconstruct:

- who authorized;
- what system/version;
- what target category;
- what limits;
- what warnings;
- what intervention;
- what changes.

### Speech line

> “Accountability requires evidence, not just responsibility written on paper.”

---

# 13. INCIDENT LEARNING

Proposed Brazil mechanism:

**Incident → Preserve → Investigate → Correct → Retest → Re-review → Reauthorize**

### Speech line

> “An incident should change future assurance, not simply produce a report.”

---

# 14. VERIFICATION

Use a graduated model:

1. national process;
2. general reporting;
3. expert exchange;
4. incident reporting;
5. protected technical review;
6. narrower agreed inspection where appropriate.

### Speech line

> “Verify what must be demonstrated without unnecessarily exposing what should remain confidential.”

---

# 15. PROCUREMENT

SIPRI's 2026 work identifies procurement as an implementation mechanism for responsible military AI and discusses clearer supplier expectations.

### Brazil use

> “Governance should begin before deployment.”

---

# 16. CAPACITY-BUILDING

Possible:

- legal-review assistance;
- technical testing;
- cybersecurity;
- training;
- regional assurance hubs;
- expert exchange.

### Speech line

> “Universal obligations need realistic pathways to implementation.”

---

# 17. BRAZIL 2025 TECHNICAL PAPER

Brazil's 2025 CCW/GGE technical paper on IEEE 7007 discusses technical standards as a possible building block for confidence-building and governance of autonomous weapons systems, including control and operating constraints.

### Important caveat

IEEE 7007 is a technical reference / possible building block, not a universally adopted LAWS treaty standard.

### Better speech use

Use it as proof that Brazil engages with the **technical governance side** of the issue, not as the foundation of the entire GSL.

# 21. HOW TO USE THE VOTE TREND

A good line:

> “Three successive General Assembly resolutions have attracted large majorities on LAWS while specialist negotiations in the CCW have continued.”

Do not claim that the vote totals represent support for one identical treaty.

# 22. CURRENT BRAZIL POSITION

Brazil's working position:

- legally binding international instrument as intended destination;
- prohibition + strict regulation;
- effective/context-appropriate human judgement/control;
- continuing accountability;
- legal review;
- testing;
- lifecycle assurance;
- capacity-building;
- GGE work as a practical negotiating basis.

### Speech rule

Pick 2–3 of these per speech.

---

# 23. COUNTRY POSITION USE

For GSL, country positions should be used as:

> “This delegation may be able to work with us on X.”

Not:

> “Country X agrees with Brazil on everything.”

Especially distinguish:

- procedural convergence;
- substantive convergence;
- legal-form convergence.

---

# 24. CASE-STUDY DISCIPLINE

Before naming any real-world case, know:

- what is documented;
- what is alleged;
- what is disputed;
- what source supports it;
- whether it is actually LAWS rather than AI decision-support.

### Safe pattern

> “The case illustrates an evidence/accountability problem, but aspects of the system's autonomy or role remain disputed.”

Avoid making a disputed case the backbone of GSL 1.
