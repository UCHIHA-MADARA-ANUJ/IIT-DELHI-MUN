# 02 — GSL TARGETING MAP

> “Targeting” here means **who should hear which argument and who we should approach after speaking**.
>
> It does NOT mean attacking countries.

The first speech should primarily target:

### BROAD ROOM

Everyone should understand:

# 4. FIRST MOD TARGETING

After the opening GSL:

### If bridge states are talking

Raise:

> “What practical safeguards can States agree on regardless of legal form?”

### If treaty-oriented states are talking

Raise:

> “Which autonomous systems or uses should be prohibited?”

### If technical states are talking

Raise:

> “How should testing and predictability be demonstrated?”

### If Global South delegates are active

Raise:

> “How can implementation capacity be built across States with different technical resources?”

# 21. THE TARGETING CYCLE

For each speech:

**SPEAK**

↓

**WATCH WHO REACTS**

↓

**APPROACH THOSE DELEGATES**

↓

**ASK ONE CONCRETE QUESTION**

↓

**GET ONE CLAUSE**

↓

**RECORD IT**

This is more important than delivering a perfect speech and doing nothing afterward.

---

# 22. WHAT TO WATCH FOR IN THE ROOM

### If a delegate repeats your phrase

They may be adopting your framing.

### If a delegate asks for your mechanism

High-interest signal.

### If a delegate challenges the exact wording

Potential drafting partner.

### If a delegate attacks the legal form but accepts safeguards

Bridge opportunity.

### If a delegate asks about implementation

Bring in capacity-building.

### If a delegate asks about verification

Bring in protected review.

---

# 23. THE BEST GSL TARGETING SIGNAL

The strongest response after a speech is not applause.

It is:

> “Can you send me that clause?”

That means the speech produced drafting interest.

---

# 24. “ATTACK” POLICY

### Early

Do not directly attack.

### Middle

Challenge contradictions.

### Late

Directly rebut if necessary.

The preferred format:

> **Position → problem → question → Brazil alternative**

Example:

> “Some delegations argue existing IHL alone is sufficient. Brazil accepts IHL as the foundation; the question is how compliance is demonstrated after a critical software change. Our answer is material-change reassessment.”

This is stronger than:

> “You are wrong.”

---

# 25. GSL + BLOC TARGETING

The GSL should make the bloc easier to build.

The sequence:

**GSL**

> “Operating conditions should be bounded.”

**Lobby**

> “Would you support geographic and temporal limits?”

**Bloc**

> “Let's draft the operating-envelope clause.”

**Resolution**

> operative clause.

The speech is therefore the first step of the drafting process.

# 27. IMPORTANT — DO NOT TURN THIS INTO “OUR SIDE VS THEIR SIDE”

A country can be:

- close on human control;
- different on legal form;
- close on testing;
- different on prohibition.

That is normal.

Track:

**CLAUSE ALIGNMENT**

not:

**COUNTRY LABELS.**

---

# 28. FINAL TARGETING MEMORY CARD

### WHO?

Who can help us draft?

### WHAT?

Which clause do they care about?

### WHY?

What problem are they trying to solve?

### HOW?

What wording can satisfy the concern?

### NEXT?

Can they help write or defend it?

---

# 29. ONE-LINE STRATEGY

> **“We are not trying to make every delegate agree with Brazil; we are trying to make the right delegates want to build Brazil's architecture with us.”**
