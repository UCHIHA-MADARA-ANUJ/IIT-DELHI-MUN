# 06 — AAWG COUNTRY ALIGNMENT

> **Purpose:** Turn the country diplomacy map into an internal bloc worksheet.
>
> This is **not a ranking**.
> It does not decide which country is "best" or "closest."
>
> It records:
>
> **documented/working position → likely overlap → likely friction → clause to test → outreach approach**
>
> **Status rule:** "Confirmed" means part of the current working AAWG list discussed in preparation. "Potential/approach" means a country being considered for inclusion or deeper coordination. The group should update this after direct conversations.

# 19. WHAT COUNTS AS REAL AAWG COMMITMENT

A country is not truly integrated into the drafting circle just because they:

- join a WhatsApp group;
- attend one meeting;
- say "sounds good";
- agree with the general theme.

Evidence of real engagement is:

- they contribute a clause;
- they propose amendments;
- they defend an agreed provision;
- they take ownership of an issue;
- they help bring another delegation;
- they help resolve a drafting dispute.

This is also the cleanest basis for deciding eventual sponsors.

# 24. THE "DON'T LOSE AUSTRALIA/JAPAN" RULE

For AAWG specifically:

Do not force Australia/Japan into immediate agreement on:

- treaty-first language;
- one fixed human-control phrase;
- one universal direct-control model.

Instead lock them into:

- testing;
- predictability;
- operational limits;
- legal review;
- deactivation;
- traceability;
- lifecycle assurance.

That keeps the bloc broad enough to negotiate with technically capable States.

# 29. AAWG DRAFTING SESSION SCRIPT

Start:

> **"Let's agree on the five things we can all defend before we argue over wording."**

Then:

> "First: scope."

> "Second: prohibited category."

> "Third: regulated category."

> "Fourth: human judgement/control."

> "Fifth: lifecycle/accountability."

Then:

> **"Anything outside those five goes into open questions rather than becoming a bloc fight."**

That is how you keep the meeting efficient.

---

# 30. WHEN THE BLOC GETS STUCK

Use this sequence:

### STEP 1
Restate what everyone agrees on.

### STEP 2
Identify the exact disputed word/threshold.

### STEP 3
Create two bracketed alternatives.

### STEP 4
Move to the next clause.

### STEP 5
Return later once the architecture is visible.

Do not let one phrase destroy a 14-clause draft.

You are ready to move from Part 5 into solution architecture when:

- AAWG has a common problem statement.
- Five common principles are written.

- Accountability is traceable.

- Capacity-building is real, not decorative.
