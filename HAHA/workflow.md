# HAHA — MUN PREP WORKFLOW

## Mission

This folder is the working command center for the final IIT Delhi MUN preparation sprint.

The objective is not to collect endless notes. The objective is to turn existing research into:
- instant country-position recall
- deep agenda understanding
- strong bloc coordination
- an original and defensible solution architecture
- prepared speeches, MODs, POIs and rebuttals
- practical drafting ability
- repeated mock-committee practice
- a final Brazil command center for committee

Core sequence:

BRAZIL → AGENDA → LAW/TECH → COUNTRIES → BLOC → SOLUTION → SPEAKING → POIs → DRAFTING → MOCK → POLISH

---

# PART 0 — FULL RESEARCH PASS

Before each HAHA stage, go back through the relevant material in RESEARCH. The research folder remains the full source base; HAHA is the compressed committee-performance layer.

We are not treating the command sheet as a substitute for reading the research. The workflow is:

**RESEARCH → VERIFY → DISTIL → PRACTICE → UPDATE**

The full research pass covers:
- RESEARCH root files
- RESEARCH/AGENDA 00–34
- RESEARCH/BRAZIL - RESEARCH
- RESEARCH/OTHER COUNTRIES RESEARCH and its exhaustive country dossiers
- primary-source library / source ledger

Use the most relevant research first for each stage, then return to the wider library when a gap, contradiction, or new committee question appears.

## 05 — AAWG BLOC POSITION

### Goal
Turn the WhatsApp group from a collection of delegates into a bloc with an actual shared baseline.

### Current identity
Autonomy & Accountability Working Group (AAWG)

Working description:

A cross-regional bloc working together on practical regulations for LAWS, with human control, accountability and IHL at the core.

### Immediate task
Get the bloc to agree on roughly 4–5 common principles.

Possible areas to test for common ground:
1. unacceptable autonomous uses should face clear prohibitions;
2. other autonomous systems should be strictly regulated;
3. human judgement/control and continuing human responsibility must remain central;
4. legal review and testing should operate across the lifecycle;
5. states need accountability, cooperation and implementation mechanisms.

These are working areas, not pre-decided final bloc wording. The bloc should shape the final language together.

### Bloc goal
Avoid becoming:
- merely an "ethics" group
- a geography-based bloc
- a huge WhatsApp group with no common document
- a list of countries with no drafting strategy

Become:
- cross-regional
- substantively coherent
- practical
- drafting-ready

# PART IV — HOW WE WORK IN THIS FOLDER

## Rule 1 — Research must become usable
No giant dumps of facts without explaining how they help Brazil.

## Rule 2 — Evidence before confidence
For current political/diplomatic claims, verify the source and time period.

## Rule 3 — Separate three things
Always distinguish:
- documented state position
- source/analyst interpretation
- our own MUN strategy

## Rule 4 — No fake consensus
A country voting YES on a UNGA resolution does not automatically mean it agrees with every substantive clause.

## Rule 5 — Do not confuse similar concepts
Examples:
- LAWS ≠ every AI-enabled military system
- human control ≠ one single universally agreed technical definition
- Article 36 review ≠ one-time approval for every future software change
- CCW consensus ≠ a simple "one-state veto"

## Rule 6 — The bloc is collaborative
Brazil should help lead the architecture, but AAWG language should reflect real common ground.

## Rule 7 — No preparation for preparation's sake
Every note must answer:
Can I use this in a speech, POI, negotiation, drafting session, or defense?

## Rule 8 — Practice beats rereading
Once the core research is solid, move into speaking and simulation.

# SUCCESS CHECKLIST

Before committee, Brazil should be able to:

- explain its LAWS position in under 60 seconds;
- explain why its preferred framework is needed;
- defend each major mechanism;
- answer legal and technical POIs;
- identify where key countries overlap or disagree;
- explain AAWG's common ground;
- propose useful MODs;
- contribute to operative clauses;
- negotiate without abandoning Brazil's core position;
- defend the draft under pressure;
- improvise when the committee goes off-plan.

## Final principle

Do not try to win by sounding like the most experienced delegate in the room.

Win preparation by being the delegate who:
knows the issue, knows Brazil, knows the room, proposes concrete solutions, speaks clearly, negotiates intelligently, and can defend what they drafted.

That is what this folder is for.
