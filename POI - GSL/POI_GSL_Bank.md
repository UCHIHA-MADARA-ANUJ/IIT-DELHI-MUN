## OFFENSIVE POIs - BY COUNTRY

### VS USA (Your best target - big tech power, vulnerable on contradiction)

**POI 1:**
> "The US voted AGAINST the 2025 LAWS resolution with Russia and Israel while 164 states voted for, and its own Department of Defense Directive 3000.09 requires human judgment over use of force - so does the US believe its own directive is unnecessary, or that international law should not reflect the standard it already applies nationally?"

- **Weakness targeted:** Contradiction between domestic human-control requirement and opposition to international rule
- **Likely answer:** "US policy supports responsible AI, we have robust legal reviews, CCW is appropriate forum, no need for new treaty, existing IHL sufficient"
- **Follow-up:** "If CCW is sufficient, why has 11 years of GGE produced no mandate while autonomy is already used in Ukraine and Gaza? Is 11 more years what the US means by responsible?"
- **How to turn:** In next speech: "The US admits it requires human judgment nationally but denies the world the same guarantee - that is not leadership, that is exception."

**POI 2:**
> "The US Replicator program invests over a billion in autonomous systems - can the delegate assure this committee none will be capable of autonomous targeting of humans without meaningful control, and if so why oppose a prohibition that would only ban what the US says it doesn't do?"

**POI 3 (if USA says 'need definition'):**
> "Brazil uses the ICRC definition adopted by many states: system that selects and engages without human intervention after activation - plus IEEE 7007 standard Brazil tabled. Which part of that definition does the US find indefinable, or is indefinability the point?"

### VS RUSSIA

**POI 1:**
> "Russia voted against 166 states in Dec 2024, alongside only Belarus and DPRK, and has blocked GGE consensus for years while deploying the S-70 Okhotnik and Lancet in Ukraine - does Russia consider consensus to mean one state may veto 124, and if not, what text would Russia actually accept to start negotiations?"

- **Weakness:** Isolation (3 vs 166), consensus abuse, fielding systems while blocking rules
- **Likely answer:** "Russia respects CCW consensus tradition, opposes politicization, Western double standards, need to study technology"
- **Follow-up:** "Brazil presided over the GGE - inclusivity is valued, but inclusivity without outcome is paralysis. Does Russia propose a timeline, or indefinite study while deployment continues?"
- **Turn:** "Russia cannot name a text it would accept - because any rule constrains what it already deploys."

**POI 2:**
> "Russia's own 2021 statement said IHL must be respected - does Russia agree that a system that cannot distinguish combatant from civilian, as required by distinction, would be illegal, and therefore should be prohibited?"

## DEFENSIVE POIs - WHAT THEY WILL ASK BRAZIL

### ATTACK 1: Cluster Munitions Hypocrisy
**Q:** "Brazil lectures on LAWS ban but refuses to join Convention on Cluster Munitions - Avibras profits from cluster munitions. Isn't this selective morality - ban what you don't have, protect what you make?"

- **Safe answer (30 sec):**
> "Brazil is party to all CCW Protocols including Protocol V on explosive remnants, and regulates cluster munitions under IHL. Our position was about strengthening the inclusive CCW versus fragmented Oslo process with loopholes like Article 21. On LAWS, Brazil applies the same logic but has learned delay is costly - hence we now lead the 42-state push for a CCW protocol before proliferation locks in."

- **Strong answer (add):**
> "And Brazil has never used cluster munitions. The cost-benefit that allowed our landmine ban was low economic cost but high reputational gain - we acknowledge that. On LAWS, Brazil's cost is also our Amazon surveillance tech - we accept standard IEEE 7007 that will constrain even our own dual-use systems. That is consistency."

- **One-line comeback:** "Brazil regulates what it has and bans what can never comply - cluster munitions under IHL, LAWS that target humans never - that is coherence, not cherry-picking."

### ATTACK 2: Harpia / Elbit / SISFRON - You develop autonomy
**Q:** "Brazil's Harpia joint venture with Israeli Elbit develops Falcão UAVs for SISFRON border surveillance - isn't Brazil developing autonomous targeting while preaching prohibition?"

- **Safe:**
> "Falcão is a reconnaissance and surveillance platform - human-in-the-loop, under human command, subject to Article 36 review, for Amazon border monitoring. Brazil's 2024 submission insists on space-time limits and deactivation for ALL systems - including ours. We invite verification."

- **Strong (add):**
> "The partnership's domestic shareholding proves Brazil seeks sovereign control precisely to ensure human control. We tabled IEEE 7007 to hold ourselves to the same standard we ask of others. The question is whether the delegate can say the same."

- **Comeback:** "Surveillance with a human is not targeting without one."

### ATTACK 3: BRICS Block - You sit with blockers
**Q:** "Brazil is BRICS with Russia and China who oppose treaty - are you speaking for BRICS or for yourself, and can we trust Brazil to vote against its BRICS partners?"

- **Safe:**
> "Article 4 of Brazil's Constitution mandates independent foreign policy - alliances yes, submission never. Brazil voted FOR 166-state resolution Russia voted AGAINST. Our joint statement was with Ireland, Norway, Switzerland - not BRICS - proving principle over bloc."

- **Strong:**
> "BRICS is economic, not a military alliance. On LAWS, Brazil leads the 42-state cross-regional group. That bridge role is why Brazil sought GGE presidency in 2022-2023 - to connect Global South and North."

- **Comeback:** "Brazil's autonomy is the point - we differ from BRICS here as we differ from the US elsewhere."

### ATTACK 4: No LAWS capability = cheap talk
**Q:** "Brazil has no LAWS, so ban costs you nothing. Tech powers pay price - why should they listen to a non-developer defining their weapons?"

- **Safe:**
> "Prevention costs less than proliferation. Brazil knows dual-use AI is cheap - even non-state actors will access it soon. If only developers write rules, rules will be written after deployment, as with landmines. Brazil offers technical standard IEEE 7007 and legal experience, not just morals - 166 states agree."

- **Strong:**
> "And 62 percent of Brazilians oppose killer robots - this is Global South public opinion, not elite Geneva talk. The 42-state statement is led by Brazil precisely because non-developers have legitimacy to demand rules before race locks in."

- **Comeback:** "The cheapest time to regulate is before you build the weapon."

### ATTACK 5: Forum shopping - CCW vs UNGA
**Q:** "For cluster munitions Brazil said CCW is the only legitimate forum and boycotted Oslo - now for LAWS you want UNGA bypass. Isn't that hypocrisy?"

- **Safe:**
> "Brazil still wants a CCW protocol - our March 2026 statement calls the CCW rolling text the basis. But Brazil's Explanation of Vote 2023 said UNGA informal consultations give fresher views from wider audience. Inclusivity without outcome is delay, and the window is closing."

- **Comeback:** "Brazil wants CCW to succeed - UNGA is insurance if one state treats consensus as veto."

### ATTACK 6: Definition impossible / Will stifle innovation
**Q:** "You can't define AWS - every definition either bans all AI or bans nothing. Will Brazil kill innovation?"

- **Safe:**
> "Brazil uses ICRC definition - system that selects and engages without human intervention after activation - plus functional tests: can it be understood, predicted, directed, limited? If not, prohibit. If yes, regulate with human approval and IEEE standard. That's definable and testable, not vague."

- **Comeback:** "If you can build it, you can define it enough to review it under Article 36 - you already do."

## POI TECHNIQUE FOR INDIAN MUN

- **Stand straight, name delegate, be short (15-20 sec).** Example: "Delegate of USA, Brazil notes..." not "Would the delegate like to explain..."
- **One question, one weakness.** Don't ask two.
- **After answer, don't argue - use follow-up in NEXT speech:** "The delegate of Russia could not name a text it would accept - that proves blocking."
- **Chits help POIs:** Send chit to chair after your POI: "Follow-up on USA contradiction: DoD directive 3000.09" - chair remembers.
