# UN LAWS RECORD — 2023–2026

**Purpose:** factual backbone for Brazil's research before lobbying. This file records the real UN/CCW process only. It is **not** a GSL, MOD plan, bloc plan, or resolution draft.

## 2. What changed from 2024 to 2025

The 2025 vote remained overwhelmingly positive, but the opposition group expanded. The six NO votes were **Belarus, Burundi, Democratic People's Republic of Korea, Israel, Russian Federation and United States**. The seven abstentions were **Argentina, China, Estonia, Fiji, Iran, Nicaragua, Poland, Romania, Saudi Arabia, Türkiye** — note that the displayed UN record must be checked for the exact voting/non-voting classification when quoting individual states; the headline vote is authoritative at 164–6–7.

**Important:** Do not describe a country as a permanent opponent merely from one vote. Use the vote as a research lead and then verify its substantive statements.

## 3. 2025 resolution: substantive content

A/RES/80/57:

- calls for a comprehensive and inclusive multilateral approach;
- addresses legal, technological, ethical, humanitarian and security dimensions;
- stresses the urgency of addressing autonomous weapons in the CCW context;
- calls on CCW High Contracting Parties to work toward completing the elements of an instrument, with a view to future negotiations;
- stresses the role of humans in the use of force, responsibility and accountability;
- recognizes the importance of international cooperation and capacity-building, including meaningful participation of developing countries;
- takes note of the Secretary-General's call for a legally binding instrument based on a two-tier approach of prohibitions and regulations.

**Primary source:** https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4095989/files/A_RES_80_57-EN.pdf

## 4. 2026 CCW process

The 2026 CCW GGE mandate is to further consider and formulate, **by consensus**, elements of an instrument, **without prejudging its nature**, and other possible measures addressing emerging technologies in the area of LAWS.

The 2026 meetings were scheduled for **2–6 March** and **31 August–4 September 2026**. The second session therefore occurred immediately before this MUN's September 2026 date.

The March 2026 session worked directly from the Chair's **rolling text**, circulated in December 2025. The GGE report records substantive, technical and detailed discussions on that text.

**Primary source:** https://docs-library.unoda.org/Convention_on_Certain_Conventional_Weapons_-Group_of_Governmental_Experts_on_Lethal_Autonomous_Weapons_Systems_%282026%29/CCW-GGE.1-2026-WP.2.pdf

## 5. 2025 draft resolution sponsors

Brazil was an original sponsor of A/C.1/80/L.41 together with Austria, Belgium, Costa Rica, Cuba, Guatemala, Ireland, Kazakhstan, Kiribati, Liechtenstein, Malta, Mexico, New Zealand, Philippines, Republic of Moldova, San Marino, Sierra Leone, Sri Lanka, Switzerland, Tonga and Venezuela.

Additional sponsors included Armenia, Barbados, Cabo Verde, Colombia, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Honduras, Trinidad and Tobago, Vanuatu and the State of Palestine.

## 6. Research implications for Brazil

These facts create a useful starting point, but they do **not** determine our MUN bloc or MODs.

Before lobbying, research should answer:

1. Which matrix states have consistently supported the UN process?
2. Which states changed their vote between 2024 and 2025?
3. Which states sponsored the 2025 draft?
4. Which states have substantive national/CCW positions that align with Brazil?
5. Which states support a legally binding instrument, and what legal form do they prefer?
6. Which states focus primarily on human control, IHL, prohibition, regulation, verification, definitions, security or development?
7. Which apparently compatible states have reservations that could matter during bloc formation?

**Rule:** voting alignment is a starting signal, not proof of full policy alignment.
