# SOURCE QUALITY & VERIFICATION — BRAZIL DISEC LAWS

## Source hierarchy

### Tier 1 — Primary
- UN Digital Library
- UNODA / CCW documents
- official national foreign ministry/permanent mission statements
- official government submissions
- official voting records
- treaty databases and treaty texts

### Tier 2 — Authoritative legal/humanitarian
- ICRC
- ICJ / official international legal material
- official treaty bodies and parliamentary records when they document policy

### Tier 3 — Research institutions
- SIPRI
- academic journals
- university research centres
- Carnegie
- RSIS
- CIGI
- Chatham House and comparable institutions

### Tier 4 — Monitoring / advocacy
- Automated Decision Research
- Reaching Critical Will
- HRW
- Article 36
- Campaign to Stop Killer Robots

These are useful for mapping positions and locating primary documents. A high-stakes claim about an official state position should be checked against the underlying state document whenever possible.

### Tier 5 — Reporting
- Reuters and other reputable current reporting

## Source record

Each high-value source should record:
- title;
- institution;
- date;
- document number;
- URL;
- exact page/paragraph where relevant;
- primary/secondary classification;
- claim supported;
- whether the source is current enough for September 2026.

## Contradiction protocol

When sources differ:
1. prefer the latest primary state/UN record;
2. preserve the earlier record to show evolution;
3. describe the evolution explicitly;
4. never silently replace an old position with a new one.

## Voting protocol

Record:
- resolution;
- date;
- YES/NO/ABSTAIN/NV;
- explanation of vote where available;
- sponsor status where available.

A vote is a signal about a specific resolution, not proof of a complete negotiating platform.

## Current-position protocol

A current position should normally be supported by a 2026 statement if one exists. Otherwise use 2025, then 2024, with the date stated clearly.

## “Weakness” protocol

For this research project, “weakness” means a **documented constraint, unresolved policy difference, implementation problem, contradictory record, or negotiating limitation**.

Do not invent scandals or personal attacks.

The underlying evidence must always be visible so the delegate can decide whether and how to use the point.
