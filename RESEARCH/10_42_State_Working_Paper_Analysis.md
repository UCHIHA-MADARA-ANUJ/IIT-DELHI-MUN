# 42-STATE CCW WORKING-PAPER GROUP — EXACT RESEARCH
> Primary source: CCW/MSP/2025/WP.5, submitted by Brazil, Belgium, Ireland, Norway and Switzerland on 26 November 2025.

## 1. The exact institutional distinction

The paper says it was submitted on behalf of **42 High Contracting Parties (HCPs)**.

Separately, **Kiribati and Thailand were observer States that associated themselves with the working paper**.

That means the phrase “42-state group” should not be used to count Thailand or Kiribati as one of the 42 HCPs.

## 4. What the paper actually says

The signatories:
- thanked the GGE Chair for the revised rolling text;
- described the rolling text as a sufficient basis to fulfil the GGE mandate in its current form;
- characterized the text as a coherent and well-structured set of elements of an instrument;
- supported continued work on the text.

This is evidence of support for using the rolling text as a basis for the GGE's work and possible negotiations. It is **not identical to an unconditional statement that all 42 states accept every operative provision or one identical legal formula**.

## 5. Exact committee intersection

Of the 42 HCPs, the following are in our 86-country MUN matrix:

Thailand is also in our matrix, but is an **observer associate**, not one of the 42 HCPs.

Kiribati is not in our matrix.

## 6. Research implication

This group is important evidence of cross-regional support for the rolling-text process. It should **not** be converted automatically into a Brazil bloc.

For each matrix state in the group, research still needs to establish:
- exact legal-form preference;
- preferred prohibitions;
- preferred regulations;
- human-control language;
- verification/lifecycle provisions;
- Article 36 position;
- technology-transfer/capacity-building priorities;
- what compromises the state would require.

## 7. September 2026 update — 47-state statement

The 2025 42-HCP paper remains the correct institutional record for the 42-state group.

However, it should no longer be treated as the newest procedural signal. Monitoring of the final 2026 GGE session reports that Brazil delivered an updated joint statement supported by 47 States, continuing to support the GGE elements as a basis for negotiations within the CCW.

Four reported new endorsers were Bangladesh, Cyprus, Greece and Malta.

In the 86-country MUN matrix, the reported 2026 endorsers are:
Algeria, Bangladesh, Brazil, Chile, Denmark, Ecuador, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Kazakhstan, Malta, Mexico, New Zealand, Nigeria, Norway, Pakistan, Peru, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland and Thailand.

This is a **2026 procedural convergence signal**, not proof of identical treaty preferences.
