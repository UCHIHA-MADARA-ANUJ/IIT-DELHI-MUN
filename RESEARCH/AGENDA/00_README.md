
# AGENDA — Full Research Dossier
## Establishing an International Regulatory Framework for the Development and Deployment of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems

Committee: UNGA DISEC / First Committee  
Country: Brazil  
Research cutoff: 21 September 2026  
Status: agenda-first research; bloc and resolution strategy not yet locked.

This folder isolates the agenda itself from Brazil country research and from bloc strategy.

## Core research questions

1. What exactly is a lethal autonomous weapons system?
2. What is already deployed, what is emerging, and what remains speculative?
3. Which IHL rules already apply?
4. Where are the practical legal and governance gaps?
5. What must human judgement and control mean in practice?
6. Which systems or uses might require prohibition?
7. Which systems could be regulated through operational limits?
8. How does accountability survive autonomous behaviour?
9. How should development, procurement, testing, deployment, updating and retirement be governed?
10. What can realistic transparency and verification look like?
11. What are the security, escalation, proliferation and arms-race implications?
12. What has the CCW GGE actually agreed, and what remains contested?
13. Which international legal and institutional models exist?
14. Where do States converge on individual provisions, rather than broad labels?

## Evidence standard

Every important statement should be treated as one of four types:
- existing international law;
- negotiated/intergovernmental text;
- State position;
- analytical inference.

Never present a State proposal, ICRC recommendation or think-tank analysis as settled international law.

## Current 2026 context

The 2026 CCW GGE completed its three-year mandate with consensus adoption of its final report on 4 September 2026. The Preparatory Committee for the Seventh CCW Review Conference met 7–9 September 2026. The Review Conference is scheduled for 16–20 November 2026.

The GGE mandate was to formulate elements of an instrument without prejudging its legal nature. It was not itself a treaty-negotiation mandate.
