
# 06 — Lifecycle, Accountability and Verification

## 1. Lifecycle governance
Autonomy can change after procurement through:
- software updates;
- model retraining;
- new target data;
- altered mission parameters;
- new operating environments.

A framework that checks a weapon only once can therefore become obsolete.

## 2. Recommended lifecycle sequence for analysis

Research → Development → Legal review → Procurement → Testing → Deployment → Operation → Modification → Retirement/transfer control.

## 3. Procurement
SIPRI's 2026 research treats procurement as an important mechanism for implementing international commitments.

Possible procurement requirements:
- technical documentation;
- safety/testing evidence;
- update notification;
- auditability;
- cybersecurity;
- supplier incident reporting;
- configuration management.

## 4. Article 36 plus technical assurance
Legal review asks whether a weapon can lawfully be employed.

Testing asks whether it actually performs within the assumptions used in that review.

Both are therefore relevant.

## 5. Accountability
Potential actors:
- State authorities;
- senior commanders;
- mission planners;
- operators;
- procurement authorities;
- developers/manufacturers.

Do not assume each actor has the same legal responsibility. The point is to make relevant duties identifiable.

## 6. Traceability
The 2026 GGE debate raised:
- traceable human command/control;
- data logs;
- audit trails;
- lifecycle records.

The purpose is not public disclosure of classified operations. The purpose is preserving enough evidence for lawful review and investigation.

## 7. Testing
Useful testing areas:
- realistic environments;
- degraded sensors;
- communications failure;
- unexpected civilian presence;
- software/model updates;
- hostile interference;
- interactions with other systems.

## 8. Verification ladder

Level 1: national implementation and legal review.

Level 2: transparency about national frameworks.

Level 3: serious-incident reporting.

Level 4: expert peer exchange.

Level 5: retention of auditable records.

Level 6: international verification or inspection where politically and operationally feasible.

The higher the level, the more sovereignty and national-security issues arise.

## 9. CCW experience
CCW Protocol V provides a concrete example of recurring national reporting and a public database for implementation information.

A LAWS framework could adapt the reporting principle while keeping sensitive technical information protected.

## 10. Industry and suppliers
A framework should anticipate:
- black-box procurement;
- proprietary models;
- update cycles;
- supplier obligations;
- cybersecurity;
- evidence retention.

## 11. Material modification
A key unresolved policy question is when a software/model change becomes sufficiently significant to trigger renewed legal review.

A workable approach is to focus on material changes affecting:
- target selection;
- effects;
- operating conditions;
- autonomy level;
- safety/assurance assumptions.
