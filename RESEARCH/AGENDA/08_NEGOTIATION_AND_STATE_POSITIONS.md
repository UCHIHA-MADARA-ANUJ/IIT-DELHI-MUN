
# 08 — Negotiation and State Position Landscape

This is an issue map, not an ally/enemy ranking.

## Pakistan
2024 proposal:
- functional scope;
- proposed CCW Protocol VI;
- prohibit systems taking force decisions without required human control/predictability;
- prohibit systems inherently incapable of IHL compliance;
- restrict targets to military objectives by nature;
- intervention, interruption and deactivation;
- time/geographic/scale limits;
- restrictions on autonomous changes to targeting parameters.

## India
2025:
- IHL applies fully;
- human judgement and oversight are essential;
- autonomy is a continuum;
- prohibit fully autonomous LAWS that cannot comply with IHL;
- regulate other LAWS for IHL compliance;
- distinguishes existing human-in/on-loop systems from fully autonomous systems.

## China
2025 military-AI paper:
- people-centred approach;
- human as final subject of responsibility;
- human control;
- IHL compliance;
- security/reliability/controllability;
- avoid arms races and miscalculation;
- agile governance.

This supports discussion of responsible military-AI governance but does not by itself prove support for one specific treaty model.

## Russia
2024 CCW paper:
- emphasizes IHL;
- resists treating meaningful human control as a standalone legal category;
- supports lifecycle management, realistic testing, reliability and operator monitoring;
- stresses prevention of non-State access.

## France
2024 CCW materials show a contextual approach in which not every autonomous operation requires constant real-time human intervention, while systems that cannot comply with IHL are unacceptable.

## UK
UK policy says it does not possess fully autonomous weapons and has no intention of developing them, while also emphasizing lawful and ethical AI use.

At the same time, UK defence strategy has strongly increased investment in autonomous systems and drones during 2025–2026. This makes UK policy a useful example of the difference between regulating a technology and rejecting military autonomy altogether.

## United States
The 2023 Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI and Autonomy includes:
- legal reviews;
- senior oversight;
- auditability;
- explicit uses;
- rigorous testing across the lifecycle;
- safeguards and deactivation.

It is nonbinding and comes from the previous administration, so do not silently treat it as the entire current 2026 U.S. position.

## Sri Lanka
On 7 September 2026, Sri Lanka called for:
- an explicit negotiating mandate;
- a legally binding instrument;
- human judgement/control across the lifecycle;
- prohibitions on systems outside human control;
- prohibition of direct autonomous targeting of humans.

## European Union
The EU's September 2026 PrepCom statement stressed the CCW's role as an IHL instrument and the need for an ambitious substantive future-work outcome. Its statement should be read as an institutional position, not as proof that every EU member shares every LAWS clause identically.

## The correct country-comparison axes

Do not ask only "pro or anti LAWS?"

Ask:
1. legal form;
2. scope;
3. prohibited systems/uses;
4. human control standard;
5. operational restrictions;
6. lifecycle obligations;
7. verification/transparency;
8. capacity-building;
9. transfer/proliferation controls.
