
# 11 — Research Gaps Before Bloc Design

## 1. Exact public 2026 GGE final text
The GGE adopted its final report by consensus on 4 September 2026. Before quoting exact paragraph wording in speeches or a draft resolution, verify the final official document in the UN document system rather than relying on transcripts or third-party summaries.

## 2. November negotiating mandate
Track official PrepCom and Review Conference documents for:
- treaty/protocol mandate;
- negotiating timetable;
- intersessional work;
- institutional structure;
- relationship between GGE elements and future negotiations.

## 3. All 86 committee States
Continue normalizing the country matrix against the agenda variables:
- legal form;
- scope;
- prohibition categories;
- human control;
- operational restrictions;
- lifecycle;
- verification;
- capacity-building;
- transfers/non-State actors.

## 4. Verification
Need further work on:
- classified information;
- audits;
- software/model assurance;
- international inspections;
- third-party review;
- secure record sharing.

## 5. AI decision-support boundary
Research where future regulation should draw the line between:
- LAWS;
- autonomous systems;
- AI-enabled decision support;
- uncrewed systems;
- military cyber/ICT.

## 6. Export controls
Compare interaction with:
- CCW;
- Arms Trade Treaty;
- national export controls;
- other technology-control arrangements.

## 7. Industry responsibility
Need more primary evidence on:
- supplier duties;
- software updates;
- contractual assurance;
- technical documentation;
- liability/accountability allocation.

## 8. Non-State actors
Separate documented evidence from theoretical proliferation scenarios.

## 9. Current State language
Where a position is older than 2025, label it historical and verify whether the State has issued a 2026 update.

## 10. Brazil-specific next step
Once this agenda folder and the 86-country matrix are complete, build a component-by-component overlap table for Brazil.

Only then should the bloc be designed.
