
# 12 — 2026 GGE Final-Report Audit

## Status as of 21 September 2026

The CCW Group of Governmental Experts on LAWS held its final two mandated sessions on 2–6 March and 31 August–4 September 2026.

The Group completed its mandate and adopted its final report by consensus on 4 September 2026. The official meeting record and final-session transcript make clear that the report was intended to provide a basis for future work under a mandate to be determined by the Seventh Review Conference.

### Critical legal/procedural caveat

The GGE mandate was to formulate a set of elements of an instrument **without prejudging its nature**.

Therefore:

- the GGE outcome is not itself a treaty;
- it does not by itself create new international legal obligations;
- consensus on the report does not establish that all States agree on the legal form of a future instrument;
- the November Review Conference remains the key next-step decision point.

Turkey expressly recorded that it joined consensus on the understanding that the elements were without prejudice to the nature of any future instrument and did not themselves create new legal obligations.

## Substantive architecture emerging from the GGE

The final-session discussion shows that the negotiated package covered several layers.

### 1. Characterization of LAWS

The 2026 working process used a capability-based characterization centered on a functionally integrated combination of weapons and technological components capable of identifying, selecting and engaging a target without human intervention in those execution tasks.

Important negotiated questions included:

- whether "identify" belongs in the characterization;
- whether human parameter-setting excludes a system;
- whether non-lethal effects exclude a system;
- whether the formulation could unintentionally capture existing fire-and-forget or homing systems;
- whether "lethal" should be an independent criterion.

The Chair described the characterization in the final session as a **working characterization** rather than a permanent universal definition.

### 2. Applicability of international law

The GGE architecture continues to anchor the discussion in applicable international law, especially IHL, including:

- distinction;
- proportionality;
- precautions in attack.

This is important because the emerging framework is designed to regulate autonomy without replacing the existing legal framework.

### 3. Human judgement and control

The 2026 discussion connected human control to IHL compliance and debated:

- direct vs indirect human control;
- context;
- anticipated effects;
- intervention;
- deactivation;
- operational limits;
- responsible chain of command.

The negotiation therefore moved beyond the slogan "human control" toward conditions under which judgement and control have practical legal effect.

### 4. Human responsibility and accountability

A major principle is that responsibility/accountability cannot be transferred to machines.

The implementation discussion includes the idea of maintaining a responsible human chain of command and national mechanisms for investigation where LAWS use may involve IHL violations.

### 5. Testing and evaluation

The 2026 text discussions considered:

- understanding capabilities and limitations;
- reliability;
- identification/selection/engagement functions;
- testing and evaluation;
- simulations;
- expected operating environments;
- training, guidance and instructions.

Some delegations sought to keep these obligations tightly linked to existing international law and feasible national practice.

### 6. Traceability and records

The broader 2026 GGE work addressed:

- traceable command/control;
- data logs;
- audit trails;
- retention of relevant evidence.

The implementation challenge is to preserve accountability without requiring disclosure of sensitive operational or proprietary information.

### 7. Investigation and reporting

The negotiation included discussion of national mechanisms for investigation and measures in cases where LAWS use may involve IHL violations.

One negotiating sensitivity was whether such requirements should be described as implementing existing international obligations or as creating separate new obligations.

### 8. Future institutional work

The final-session chair said the agreed elements would provide a basis for future work under the mandate to be determined by the Seventh Review Conference.

The chair's draft-report recommendation was for the Review Conference to endorse the set of elements and decide by consensus on future work.

## MUN significance

By 26–27 September 2026, the committee should not behave as if the international community is starting from zero.

The international process has already produced:
- a working characterization;
- structured regulatory elements;
- a substantial negotiating history;
- a rolling-text methodology;
- a consensus GGE outcome.

The unresolved issue is the next legal and institutional step.
