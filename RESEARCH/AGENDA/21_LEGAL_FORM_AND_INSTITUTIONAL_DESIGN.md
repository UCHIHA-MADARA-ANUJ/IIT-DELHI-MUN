
# 21 — Deep Dive: Legal Form and Institutional Design

## The substantive rules and the forum are separate questions

A State can agree with a prohibition or restriction but disagree over:
- treaty vs political declaration;
- CCW vs standalone forum;
- timing;
- sequencing;
- whether technical work should continue before formal negotiations.

## Option A — CCW protocol

Possible structure:
- definitions/characterization;
- prohibited systems/uses;
- regulated systems;
- national implementation;
- weapons review;
- testing;
- reporting;
- review conference;
- technical expert exchange;
- assistance.

Advantages:
- existing institution;
- established humanitarian/disarmament context;
- direct link to the GGE work.

Constraints:
- consensus-based GGE negotiations can be slowed by unresolved disagreement;
- universalization and implementation still require political work.

## Option B — Standalone treaty

Potentially allows a dedicated architecture.

Challenges:
- relationship with CCW;
- duplication;
- participation;
- institutional fragmentation;
- verification and implementation.

## Option C — Political declaration first

Could:
- clarify responsible-use principles;
- build common terminology;
- establish national commitments;
- prepare later negotiations.

Limitation:
does not itself create new binding prohibitions.

## Option D — Layered architecture

Potentially:
1. binding prohibitions;
2. binding regulatory obligations;
3. national implementation;
4. technical standards/guidance;
5. reporting;
6. capacity-building;
7. review.

This is often the most conceptually complete architecture because not every requirement has to be expressed in treaty-level technical detail.

## Relationship with 2026 GGE

The GGE mandate was deliberately open regarding the nature of a future instrument.

The 2026 work therefore creates a bridge:
GGE elements → Review Conference decision → negotiation format → formal legal instrument.

The Review Conference is the institutional decision point.

## Review mechanism

A future instrument needs to survive technological change.

Potential features:
- periodic conferences;
- technical expert body;
- updates to nonbinding technical annexes;
- national reporting;
- review of material technological developments.

## Consensus and inclusivity

An international framework may seek broad participation while preserving a specialized technical process.

A possible design is:
- political decisions at the review conference;
- technical guidance by experts;
- national implementation and reporting;
- confidential expert exchanges.

## Capacity and equity

Rules that require expensive testing or cyber assurance can disproportionately burden developing States.

Possible support:
- legal-review assistance;
- regional laboratories;
- training;
- shared technical guidance;
- voluntary funding mechanism.

## Research conclusion

The institutional question should not be reduced to "CCW or UNGA." The real design problem is how to transform the GGE's elements into durable rules with broad implementation.
