# 24 — 2026 Joint-Statement Audit: The 47-State Negotiating Signal

## Current signal

Monitoring of the final 2026 GGE session records that Brazil delivered an updated joint statement supported by **47 States**. The statement continued to describe the GGE text as a sufficient basis to fulfil the mandate and as a basis for negotiations within the CCW on an instrument on LAWS.

A secondary monitoring source reports that four States newly endorsed the 2026 statement: Bangladesh, Cyprus, Greece and Malta. The same source reports that 76 States had expressed support for moving toward negotiations on an instrument based on the GGE text under discussion.

The 47-state list should be treated as a current procedural-convergence signal. Before quoting it as an official UN paper, retrieve the primary CCW document if/when it receives a permanent indexed symbol.

## Comparison with the 2025 42-HCP group

The 2025 group had 22 members inside our matrix:

Angola, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Denmark, Ecuador, France, Germany, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Kazakhstan, Mexico, New Zealand, Nigeria, Norway, Pakistan, Peru, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland.

The 2026 statement newly reaches Algeria, Bangladesh, Greece, Malta and Thailand among the committee countries, while Angola and Colombia are not reported among the 47 endorsers.

This does not prove Angola or Colombia changed position. It only means they were not listed by the monitoring source as endorsers of the 2026 statement.

## Interpretation

Support for the negotiating basis is not identical to agreement on every substantive clause or identical treaty preference.

A country can support:
- the GGE text as the negotiating basis;
- stronger restrictions;
- a different definition;
- different human-control language;
- different transfer/verification provisions;
while still joining the statement.
