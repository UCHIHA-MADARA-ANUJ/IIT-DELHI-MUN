# 25 — 86-State Convergence Clusters

## Purpose

This file bridges agenda research and bloc architecture. It groups delegations by documented convergence on specific policy components; it does not rank them.

## A. Current procedural convergence around the GGE text

The 2026 Brazil joint statement provides the clearest current procedural signal. The 25 committee delegations reported as endorsers are:

Algeria, Bangladesh, Brazil, Chile, Denmark, Ecuador, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Kazakhstan, Malta, Mexico, New Zealand, Nigeria, Norway, Pakistan, Peru, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Thailand.

## B. Earlier rolling-text participants still relevant

Angola and Colombia were in the 2025 42-HCP intersection but were not reported among the 2026 47-state endorsers. Their earlier support remains relevant but should be clarified before treating them identically to the 25 current endorsers.

## C. Binding-instrument advocates outside the current 2026 statement

The desk-research matrix contains recent or explicit binding-instrument signals from additional delegations including:

Armenia, Botswana, Cambodia, Cuba, Ghana, Kenya, Kuwait, Libya, Micronesia, Myanmar, Nepal, Philippines, South Africa, Sri Lanka, Sudan, Tanzania, Togo, Trinidad & Tobago and Tunisia.

These signals are not all equally current. Before lobbying, verify the latest national/CCW statement for each.

## D. Contextual/regulatory bridge positions

The 2026 GGE discussions show technically active States accepting much of the emerging operational package while resisting language that turns human judgement/control into a standalone new legal standard.

Examples include the United States, Russia, Republic of Korea, Australia, Canada, Japan, United Kingdom and Türkiye.

The current evidence shows engagement with:
- IHL;
- operational human judgement/control;
- contextual restrictions;
- testing/evaluation;
- lifecycle safeguards.

Their differences concern legal characterization, mandatory wording, or whether a new binding instrument should follow.

## E. Explicitly cautious legal-form positions

Türkiye stated that its consensus participation should not be read as support for a legally binding instrument and that control/human judgement references should not create new legal standards beyond existing IHL. The United States made a similar distinction, describing control and human judgement as components of IHL compliance rather than standalone legal rules. Russia also emphasized this distinction.

## F. Strong human-control/regulation convergence

The final GGE discussions show recurring support for:
- human judgement;
- human responsibility;
- responsible chain of command;
- predictability/reliability;
- operational limits;
- testing;
- accountability.

Brazil, Ireland, Pakistan, Mexico, Portugal, New Zealand, Sri Lanka, South Africa, Armenia, Switzerland, France and Germany provide examples of delegations expressing elements of this approach, though their legal formulations differ.

## G. Important four-dimensional map

The negotiating field should be mapped across four separate dimensions:

1. Procedural — support for using the GGE elements as a negotiating basis.
2. Substantive — preferred prohibition/restriction architecture.
3. Human control — strength and legal framing.
4. Institutional — legal form and forum.

A delegation can overlap with Brazil on some dimensions while diverging on others.
