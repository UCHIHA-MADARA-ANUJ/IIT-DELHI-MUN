# 26 — Brazil Negotiating Space Before Bloc Selection

## Purpose

This is a pre-bloc map of Brazil's documented negotiating space. It is not a final bloc recommendation and does not rank countries.

## Brazil's documented core

Current Brazil research supports:
1. a legally binding international instrument as the intended outcome;
2. prohibitions for specified unacceptable autonomous weapon systems/uses;
3. strict regulation of other systems;
4. context-appropriate human judgement and control;
5. continuing human responsibility and accountability;
6. legal review and testing;
7. lifecycle safeguards;
8. international cooperation and capacity-building;
9. the CCW rolling text as the practical negotiating foundation.

## Negotiating basis

The 2026 joint statement places 25 of our committee delegations in a current cross-regional procedural circle around the GGE text:

Algeria, Bangladesh, Brazil, Chile, Denmark, Ecuador, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Kazakhstan, Malta, Mexico, New Zealand, Nigeria, Norway, Pakistan, Peru, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Thailand.

Angola and Colombia have earlier 2025 rolling-text support and should be clarified on their September 2026 position.

## Legal form

Brazil prefers a legally binding instrument.

Binding-oriented evidence also exists across:
- Africa;
- Latin America and the Caribbean;
- Europe;
- Asia-Pacific.

The useful diplomatic point is not that these States want identical treaty text. It is that support for a binding pathway is cross-regional.

## Prohibitions plus regulation

The emerging GGE architecture and multiple State positions support a two-tier model:
- prohibit clearly unacceptable systems/uses;
- regulate remaining systems through operational and lifecycle safeguards.

The exact threshold remains negotiable.

## Human judgement/control

Brazil can distinguish:
- human judgement as the legal decision-making function;
- human control as the mechanisms through which that judgement constrains the system;
- technical safeguards as the means of making control effective.

This creates room to work with delegations that dislike the phrase "meaningful human control" but accept concrete control conditions.

## Direct vs indirect control

The September 2026 GGE discussion recognizes direct and indirect forms of control and does not equate human control with constant direct supervision.

Brazil can therefore defend:
- human judgement;
- practical effect on system operation;
- context;
- intervention/deactivation where feasible;
without requiring one identical control model for every system.

## Operational limits

The emerging text discusses:
- target types;
- scale;
- duration;
- geographical scope;
- anticipated effects;
- other operational constraints.

These are useful bridge provisions because a State can resist a new legal standard while accepting concrete safeguards tied to IHL compliance.

## Predictability, reliability, traceability and explainability

These concepts appeared in the GGE negotiations and were strongly defended by delegations including Ireland. Their exact legal effect remains negotiable, but they give the committee technically grounded common language.

## Lifecycle

Potential common ground:
- legal review;
- testing/evaluation;
- deployment controls;
- operator training;
- monitoring;
- incident investigation;
- review after material modification.

## Transfers and proliferation

Germany said in the September GGE that manufacturing, acquisition, stockpiling and transfer remained integral to its view of future negotiations even though an earlier paragraph was removed from the final draft. Armenia also raised responsible transfer and diversion concerns.

This means transfer/proliferation can be kept as a future-negotiation issue rather than abandoned.

## Capacity-building

A practical package can include:
- legal-review assistance;
- technical testing support;
- cybersecurity capacity;
- regional expert exchanges;
- procurement guidance;
- training.

## Brazil's compromise space

Strong principle → flexible implementation:

- Human judgement must remain meaningful → the form of control may be direct or indirect depending on context.
- Unacceptable systems/uses should be prohibited → the exact prohibition threshold can be drafted around IHL, predictability, human-targeting and control criteria.
- A binding instrument is the intended destination → the Review Conference can establish the negotiating mandate and process rather than pretending the treaty already exists.
- Accountability must remain human and traceable → sensitive technical information can remain confidential while States maintain evidence and reporting mechanisms.

## Key conclusion

Brazil does not need every core negotiating partner to endorse every Brazilian clause. The useful coalition space is built from shared core provisions plus clearly managed differences in implementation.
