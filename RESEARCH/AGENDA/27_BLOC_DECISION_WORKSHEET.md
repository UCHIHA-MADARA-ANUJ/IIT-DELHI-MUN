# 27 — Bloc Decision Worksheet

## Purpose

Use this after agenda research and current diplomatic verification. It is a decision aid without country rankings or scores.

## Core-partner questions

Test whether a delegation can work with Brazil on:
- a common negotiating basis;
- movement toward binding international rules;
- prohibition of specified unacceptable systems/uses;
- regulation of remaining systems;
- meaningful human judgement/control in practice;
- lifecycle testing/review/accountability;
- cross-regional drafting.

Identical wording is not required.

## Manageable differences

Potentially bridgeable through drafting:
- "human judgement and control" vs "control and human judgement";
- direct vs indirect control;
- exact prohibited-system test;
- whether safeguards are described as new rules or implementation of existing IHL;
- depth of reporting;
- treatment of confidential technical information;
- sequencing of the negotiating mandate.

## Architectural differences

More consequential:
- rejecting a binding instrument altogether;
- rejecting prohibition categories altogether;
- rejecting LAWS-specific safeguards beyond existing IHL;
- refusing to preserve practical human judgement over the use of force.

## Possible coalition structure for later consideration

### Core drafting circle
Shared:
- negotiating basis;
- prohibition + regulation;
- human judgement/control;
- lifecycle safeguards.

### Extended negotiating circle
Shares most substantive pillars but seeks modifications on:
- legal form;
- wording;
- implementation.

### Bridge circle
Resists a new legal standard or binding treaty but accepts:
- IHL;
- testing;
- legal review;
- operational limits;
- accountable human decision-making.

### Clarification circle
Current evidence is incomplete; position should be tested directly rather than inferred.

## Lobbying questions

1. Do you support using the 2026 GGE elements as the negotiating basis?
2. What legal form should the Review Conference mandate?
3. Which LAWS categories or uses should be prohibited?
4. Is direct autonomous targeting of humans acceptable?
5. Can indirect human control satisfy your approach?
6. Which operational restrictions should be mandatory?
7. Which lifecycle stages require review?
8. What records should States retain?
9. What information can be public and what should remain confidential?
10. Should transfers and non-State actors be explicitly covered?
11. What capacity-building would make implementation realistic?

## Evidence rule

Before treating a country as part of a core negotiating group:
- verify its latest statement;
- distinguish current from historical language;
- distinguish national positions from group statements;
- distinguish support for the rolling text from support for a binding instrument;
- identify its non-negotiable provisions.

## Current pre-bloc map

The research currently shows:
- a 25-country matrix intersection with the 2026 Brazil joint statement;
- additional delegations with binding-instrument or stronger-regulation signals;
- a bridge group accepting many operational safeguards while disputing new legal standards;
- an unresolved group requiring direct clarification.

This is a negotiating-field map, not a final bloc.
