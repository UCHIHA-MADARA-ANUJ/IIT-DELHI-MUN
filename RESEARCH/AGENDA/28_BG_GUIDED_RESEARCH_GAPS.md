# 28 — Background-Guide-Guided Research Gaps

## Purpose

The IITD MUN 2026 DISEC Background Guide explicitly asks delegates not to copy existing national solutions and instead use the guide as a starting point for original research and deductions.

The guide's final questions identify the research areas that should drive the committee's solution design.

## EB question → research workstream

### 1. Precise definition
Research:
- capability/function definitions;
- critical targeting functions;
- distinction between LAWS, highly automated systems, AI decision-support;
- exclusions and defensive contexts.

### 2. Human control in the targeting cycle
Research:
- surveillance;
- detection;
- identification;
- tracking;
- selection;
- engagement;
- post-engagement assessment.

Key policy issue:
At what point must human judgement have practical effect?

### 3. Dual-use verification
Research:
- military AI procurement;
- supplier obligations;
- national certification;
- legal review;
- confidentiality;
- separation between civilian AI development and weaponization.

### 4. Adaptive/autonomous learning
Research:
- Article 36 review before deployment;
- review after material modifications;
- model/version controls;
- restrictions on online learning in critical targeting functions;
- continuous testing.

### 5. Accountability
Research:
- commander responsibility;
- operator responsibility;
- developer/vendor responsibilities;
- State responsibility;
- evidence retention;
- incident investigation.

### 6. Urban distinction
Research:
- target environments;
- target-category restrictions;
- geographic/time limits;
- human confirmation;
- predictable operating envelopes.

### 7. Proportionality
Research:
- whether autonomous systems can apply context-sensitive proportionality;
- pre-programmed proportionality thresholds;
- human authorization;
- civilian-harm estimation.

### 8. Blanket vs tiered regulation
Research:
- prohibited categories;
- restricted categories;
- permitted bounded autonomy;
- context-based rules.

### 9. Automation bias
Research:
- training;
- interface design;
- mandatory override authority;
- warning/uncertainty displays;
- operator workload;
- audit trails.

### 10. Adversarial machine learning
Research:
- sensor spoofing;
- adversarial examples;
- cyber intrusion;
- electronic warfare;
- fail-safe behavior;
- degraded-mode operations.

### 11. Autonomous escalation / swarms
Research:
- swarm interaction;
- engagement-speed compression;
- escalation thresholds;
- communication loss;
- deconfliction;
- emergency abort mechanisms.

### 12. Black-box systems
Research:
- explainability;
- predictability;
- testability;
- assurance evidence;
- deployment limits for opaque models.

### 13. Diplomatic deadlock
Research:
- CCW GGE consensus;
- Review Conference mandate;
- UNGA complementary role;
- soft-law interim measures;
- sequencing toward a binding instrument.

### 14. Non-State actors
Research:
- transfer controls;
- commercial components;
- illicit procurement;
- software distribution;
- counter-proliferation cooperation.

### 15. Martens Clause
Research:
- actual legal status;
- relationship with written IHL;
- whether it independently prohibits autonomous weapons;
- arguments for and against.

## New research principle

The committee is not asking only:
"Should LAWS be banned?"

It is asking:
**"What architecture can govern the entire lifecycle of autonomy while remaining legally defensible, technologically realistic and diplomatically implementable?"**

That should be the standard for our next phase.
