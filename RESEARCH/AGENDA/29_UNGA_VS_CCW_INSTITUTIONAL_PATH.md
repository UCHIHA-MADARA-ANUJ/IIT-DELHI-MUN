# 29 — UNGA vs CCW: Institutional Path

## Why this matters for DISEC

The Background Guide asks how the international community can overcome diplomatic deadlock and what role the General Assembly should play.

The answer needs institutional precision.

## CCW

The CCW GGE is the main specialist forum where States have worked through technical/legal questions in detail.

Advantages:
- specialist expertise;
- existing LAWS mandate;
- negotiated text;
- connection to weapons law;
- Review Conference mechanism.

Limitation:
GGE work and adoption of reports under its mandate have operated by consensus.

The 2017 GGE mandate explicitly required the Group to conduct its work and adopt its report by consensus. citeturn178783search61

## UNGA

The General Assembly can:
- adopt resolutions;
- request reports;
- create political momentum;
- invite further multilateral work;
- encourage negotiation in an appropriate forum;
- create complementary discussion tracks.

Resolution 78/241 was the first UNGA resolution devoted specifically to LAWS and requested the Secretary-General to seek views and report back. citeturn178783search4turn178783search7

Resolution 80/57 continued the dedicated LAWS track in 2025. citeturn178783search5

## Important limitation

A DISEC/UNGA resolution does not itself become a binding LAWS treaty merely because the General Assembly adopts it.

Therefore a strong MUN solution can use UNGA authority to:
1. consolidate political principles;
2. call for action at the CCW;
3. request reporting;
4. establish complementary expert/technical exchanges;
5. encourage capacity-building;
while recognizing that treaty obligations arise through the relevant legal process.

## Complementary model

A realistic architecture is:

**UNGA**
→ political mandate / reporting / global principles / transparency

**CCW**
→ specialist negotiations / binding instrument / technical implementation

**National authorities**
→ legal reviews / testing / procurement / certification / incident investigation

**Technical expert mechanism**
→ standards / testing methodologies / interoperability / lessons learned

This avoids treating New York and Geneva as mutually exclusive.

## Soft law bridge

If a treaty mandate is delayed, the Assembly can support interim commitments around:
- human judgement;
- prohibited practices;
- testing;
- procurement;
- incident reporting;
- transparency.

Such measures should be explicitly described as interim political commitments, not as substitute treaty law.

## Original MUN insight

A strong DISEC resolution could therefore create a **two-track pathway**:

### Track 1 — immediate
voluntary common standards and reporting.

### Track 2 — legal
mandate preparation for a legally binding instrument in the CCW.

This addresses the guide's question about what happens if binding negotiations are politically difficult in the short term.
