# 30 — Original Regulatory Architecture Options

## Purpose

The Background Guide explicitly asks for out-of-the-box solutions. The committee should therefore move beyond "ban vs regulate" and design mechanisms.

These are neutral design options to be tested against State positions.

## 1. Autonomous Weapons Impact Assessment (AWIA)

Before development/deployment of a covered system, a State records:
- intended mission;
- autonomy functions;
- target class;
- expected operating environment;
- geographic/time limits;
- foreseeable failure modes;
- human-control arrangements;
- legal review findings;
- testing evidence.

A periodic update is triggered by material modification.

### Benefit
Turns high-level responsibility into evidence.

### Risk
Could become a paperwork exercise unless tied to deployment authorization.

## 2. Autonomy Operating Envelope

Every regulated system receives a documented operating envelope specifying:
- permitted target category;
- geographic area;
- duration;
- maximum engagement scale;
- environmental assumptions;
- communication assumptions;
- intervention/deactivation conditions.

Outside the envelope, the system must not engage.

### Benefit
Makes "human control" technically concrete.

### Risk
Requires reliable geofencing/parameter controls and careful exceptions for defensive systems.

## 3. Model / Software Change Control

A material change to:
- model weights;
- target classifier;
- engagement logic;
- sensor integration;
- autonomy mode
triggers renewed testing and legal review.

### Benefit
Addresses adaptive software and the lifecycle problem.

## 4. LAWS Registry

A confidential or tiered international registry could record:
- category of system;
- legal-review status;
- testing status;
- responsible authority;
- broad operational limitations.

Public reporting could be aggregated while sensitive technical data remains protected.

### Benefit
Creates transparency without requiring publication of source code.

## 5. Serious Incident Mechanism

States notify an international focal point after a serious autonomous-weapons incident involving:
- civilian harm;
- loss of control;
- unexpected target engagement;
- unauthorized operation;
- major software/cyber failure.

Reports could initially be confidential and later aggregated into annual lessons-learned reports.

### Benefit
Creates a feedback loop.

## 6. Protected Technical Review Panel

States can submit confidential technical evidence to accredited experts regarding:
- testing;
- operating envelopes;
- failure rates;
- model-change controls.

The panel would not receive command authority over national forces.

### Benefit
Improves assurance while protecting classified information.

## 7. Emergency Abort Standard

A regulated system should contain a tested mechanism to:
- abort;
- deactivate;
- return;
- loiter safely;
when defined conditions become invalid.

The requirement should be context-dependent: some defensive engagements may be time-critical.

## 8. Procurement Gate

A State should not procure a covered autonomous system without:
- legal-review documentation;
- testing evidence;
- supplier documentation;
- cybersecurity controls;
- change-management procedures;
- accountability assignment.

## 9. Supplier Responsibility Clause

Contracts can require suppliers to:
- disclose material system changes;
- document limitations;
- preserve audit rights;
- maintain update security;
- support incident investigation;
- prevent unauthorized model modification.

The international legal obligation remains primarily with States, but procurement can create an implementation bridge.

## 10. Confidence-Building Measures

States could exchange:
- general legal-review methodologies;
- testing concepts;
- terminology;
- aggregate incident data;
- training practices.

This may be easier to negotiate than intrusive inspections.

## 11. Regional Testing Hubs

Capacity-building could establish regional centres for:
- legal-review training;
- adversarial testing;
- cyber assurance;
- model evaluation;
- operator training.

This addresses the implementation gap for States without large military-AI laboratories.

## 12. Sunset / Review Trigger

A framework could require review when:
- a new autonomy capability changes the risk profile;
- a major incident occurs;
- material technology changes emerge;
- accumulated evidence shows existing restrictions are insufficient.

## Core architecture

A mature system could combine:

**prohibited categories**
+
**operating-envelope regulation**
+
**legal review**
+
**testing**
+
**procurement controls**
+
**change management**
+
**incident reporting**
+
**international review**
+
**capacity-building**

This is the kind of architecture that can answer the Background Guide's request for an original, actionable framework.
