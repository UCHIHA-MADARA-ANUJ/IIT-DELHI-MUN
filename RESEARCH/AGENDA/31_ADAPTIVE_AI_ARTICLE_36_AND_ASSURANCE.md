# 31 — Adaptive AI, Article 36 and Assurance

## Core problem

Traditional legal review assumes that the weapon under review can be characterized sufficiently to predict its effects in expected circumstances.

Machine-learning systems complicate that assumption.

The ICRC's 2025/2026 legal analysis states that a legal reviewer must understand the capabilities and limitations of an autonomous weapon and have sufficient confidence about its effects in normal or expected circumstances. It also states that a new review is needed when modification changes a system's function or effects, including changes resulting from machine-learning software affecting selection or engagement. citeturn384013search54turn384013search5

## Why online learning is different

If a model changes during deployment, the reviewed system may no longer be exactly the system that was tested.

Potential triggers:
- online learning;
- model updates;
- new sensor fusion;
- altered target classification;
- adversarial adaptation;
- mission-level reconfiguration.

## Possible framework

### Tier 1 — fixed, bounded autonomy
No post-deployment change to critical targeting functions without review.

### Tier 2 — adaptive but controlled
Adaptation allowed only within a validated operating envelope and with:
- update logging;
- threshold controls;
- rollback;
- revalidation.

### Tier 3 — self-modifying critical targeting
Highly restricted or prohibited where the State cannot demonstrate predictability and legal compliance.

## Assurance evidence

Require:
- model/version identity;
- test set and testing environment;
- known limitations;
- failure-mode analysis;
- operating envelope;
- update/change log;
- post-deployment monitoring;
- incident history.

## Legal-review timing

Potential triggers:
1. initial development;
2. pre-acquisition;
3. pre-deployment;
4. material software change;
5. material mission change;
6. serious incident;
7. periodic review.

## MUN drafting insight

Article 36 should not be presented as a one-time certificate.

For adaptive autonomy, legal review is better understood as a **continuing assurance obligation**.

This connects an existing legal model to emerging technology without pretending that all States share identical treaty obligations.
