# 32 — AI Decision Support, Automation Bias and Human Agency

## Why this deserves separate treatment

The Background Guide uses examples of AI-supported targeting to ask whether formal human authorization can become functionally meaningless.

Current research distinguishes AI-enabled decision-support systems (AI-DSS) from autonomous weapon systems.

SIPRI's comparative study notes that the two technologies can affect different phases of the targeting cycle and that governance may require:
- integrating AI-DSS into current AWS processes;
- establishing a dedicated AI-DSS policy track;
- or deciding not to create a targeted response yet. citeturn178783search3

## Formal vs functional human control

A human signature on an order does not automatically establish meaningful judgement.

Questions:
- Did the operator have sufficient time?
- Could they inspect the evidence?
- Were uncertainty/error indicators visible?
- Could they override the recommendation?
- Was override technically possible?
- Was the operator trained to distrust/verify the system?
- Was workload so high that approval became rubber-stamping?

## Automation bias

The regulatory target should not simply be "AI."

Instead, focus on:
**human decision quality under machine influence.**

## Possible rules

### Human-review standard
A human reviewer must have:
- relevant information;
- sufficient time;
- authority;
- technical understanding;
- capacity to reject the recommendation.

### Uncertainty display
AI systems should communicate:
- confidence;
- known limitations;
- missing data;
- out-of-distribution indicators.

### Workload controls
States should set:
- maximum recommendation volume per operator;
- escalation thresholds;
- mandatory second review for high-risk targets.

### Auditability
Retain:
- recommendation;
- information relied upon;
- human decision;
- time between recommendation and action;
- override/acceptance;
- later investigation.

## Case-study caution

The Israel Defense Forces state that Lavender and Habsora are intelligence/targeting-support tools and deny that they autonomously select targets or generate attack orders. citeturn534274search2

A UN Commission report separately discussed media allegations concerning Lavender and stressed the importance of diligent assessment, validation and higher-level approval procedures. citeturn534274search67

Therefore, in MUN debate, do not present claims about Lavender as universally settled proof of a LAWS deployment. Use it to illustrate the **governance problem of AI-mediated targeting and automation bias**.

## Key distinction

**LAWS regulation ≠ military-AI regulation as a whole.**

But a serious LAWS framework should address the interface where AI decision-support materially shapes lethal targeting decisions.
