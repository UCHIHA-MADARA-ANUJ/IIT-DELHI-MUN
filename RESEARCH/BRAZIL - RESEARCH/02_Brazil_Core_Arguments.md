### ARGUMENT 1: MEANINGFUL HUMAN CONTROL IS A LEGAL REQUIREMENT, NOT A PREFERENCE

**Core line to use:** "Brazil does not ask for new ethics. We ask for enforcement of existing law - distinction, proportionality and precaution already require meaningful human control. An autonomous system that cannot be understood, predicted or interrupted violates IHL by design."

**Build:**
- IHL already applies to future weapons (ICJ). Principles of distinction/proportionality require human judgment of context - "concrete and direct military advantage" vs civilian harm. Algorithm cannot weigh this.
- Martens Clause: dictates of public conscience prohibit delegating life/death to code.
- Brazil's submission: use of force without MHC is direct violation of ethical standards underpinning IHL.
- Support: ICRC, UNSG report (47 of 58 submissions support prohibition/regulation).

**Stat to drop:** "47 of 58 state submissions to the UNSG support prohibition or regulation - this is not a fringe campaign."

**POI target:** USA "If existing IHL is enough, why does Article 36 require you to REVIEW new weapons? And why do your own DoD principles demand human judgment? You're admitting gap."

### ARGUMENT 2: TWO-TIER INSTRUMENT - BAN THE UNACCEPTABLE, REGULATE THE REST

**Core line:** "Brazil supports the Secretary-General's two-tier approach - clear prohibitions for systems that can never comply, and strict regulations with human control for all others. Abolition and regulation are not opposites; they are partners."

**Build:**
- Tier 1 prohibitions: Systems that (a) target humans, (b) cannot be controlled/predicted/explained, (c) cannot distinguish, (d) cause superfluous injury. These must be banned outright - no verification can make them legal.
- Tier 2 regulations: All other systems - require space/time limits, situational awareness before force, ability to intervene/deactivate, bans on changing parameters without validation.
- This is what 166 states voted for in Dec 2024. It's also Brazil/Chile/Mexico outline (2021) + rolling text.

**Why it wins:** Pulls Group B (France, Germany, Japan) who fear total ban but accept regulation. Shows Brazil is moderate, not radical NGO.

**Clause language to propose:**
> *OP1: Prohibits development, production, and use of AWS that select and engage human targets without meaningful human control, or whose effects cannot be limited as required by IHL.*
> *OP2: Requires human operator to approve each engagement decision with sufficient situational awareness and ability to intervene/abort within strict geographic and temporal limits.*

### ARGUMENT 3: CONSENSUS CANNOT BE VETO - CCW PLUS UNGA

**Core line:** "Brazil presided over the GGE in 2022-2023; we value the CCW's inclusivity, but inclusivity without outcome is paralysis. 11 years, 125 parties, one blocker can stop 124 - that is not multilateralism."

**Build:**
- CCW advantage is all stakeholders present. Brazil said in 2024: "Advantage is not absolute value; it should not excuse delay." Window is closing - actual use in Libya, Gaza, Ukraine proves urgency.
- Support CCW rolling text as basis for negotiation (42-state statement March 2026) AND support UNGA informal consultations (May 2025, 2025 New York) as complementary. Brazil voted FOR both.
- Call for Review Conference 2026 to get negotiation mandate. End-2026 Guterres/ICRC deadline.

**Strategic value:** You look like you defend multilateralism while calling out blockers (USA/Russia) without naming them rudely. Isolates them.

**POI to blocker:** "Delegate of Russia voted against 166 states in 2024 - does delegate believe consensus means one state overrules 166?"

### ARGUMENT 4: TECHNICAL STANDARDS + LEGAL REVIEWS MAKE TREATY VERIFIABLE

**Core line:** "Brazil does not propose vague morals. We propose IEEE 7007-2021, national Article 36 reviews, and verifiable deactivation - a treaty you can actually implement."

**Build:**
- Brazil tabled WP.1 (2024) on IEEE STD 7007-2021 (global ontological standard for ethically driven robotics) - common language for control mechanisms, target limits, duration, geography.
- Article 36 AP I already obliges legal reviews of new weapons - but reviews are secret, inconsistent. Treaty would harmonize standards, reduce legal risk for developers and militaries.
- Propose: mandatory technical certification, logging of engagement decisions, international cooperation on verification.

**Why it wins:** Answers US/UK objection "can't define or verify." Shows Brazil is technical, not preachy.

**Clause language:**
> *OP3: Establishes technical annex based on IEEE 7007-2021 and national Article 36 reviews, requiring states to certify, log, and ensure ability to deactivate AWS; encourages transfer of verification technology to developing states.*

### ARGUMENT 5: GLOBAL SOUTH SECURITY - PREVENT ARMS RACE AND PROLIFERATION BEFORE IT LOCKS IN

**Core line:** "LAWS lower the threshold for war, accelerate escalation, and will proliferate to non-state actors. If we wait until major powers deploy, regulation will be impossible - as with landmines. Global South will pay the price."

**Build:**
- Concerns in UNGA resolutions: negative consequences on global security, risk of arms race, miscalculation, lowering threshold, proliferation to terrorists/criminal groups (Brazil submission 2024).
- Dual-use AI is cheap, diffusion fast - unlike nuclear, no natural monopoly. Delay favors proliferators.
- Brazil's interest: Amazon border surveillance (SISFRON) needs stable norms; Brazil doesn't want autonomy arms race in South America.
- Poll: 62% Brazilians oppose killer robots - this is Global South public opinion, not just Geneva elites.
- Offer: capacity building, cooperation on AI for peace (disaster response, demining) vs weaponization.

**Why it wins:** Speaks to NAM, G77, Latin America, Africa. Makes LAWS not just humanitarian but security/economic issue.

**Clause language:**
> *OP4: Calls for moratorium on development of AWS targeting humans until treaty enters into force; establishes assistance fund for states to adopt human-control verification and redirect AI to humanitarian uses.*

## WHAT YOU NEVER COMPROMISE

- Legally binding form (not just political declaration)
- Prohibition on autonomous targeting of humans
- Meaningful human control over critical functions (not just "human involvement")
- CCW rolling text as basis, not starting from zero

## WHERE YOU CAN COMPROMISE TO LEAD BLOC

- Timeline (endorse 2026 deadline but accept phased implementation)
- Exact definition (accept ICRC definition, discuss thresholds for predictability)
- Verification mechanism (offer technical annex + voluntary confidence building first)
- Forum wording: "CCW primary forum with UNGA complementary consultations" - keeps Group B comfortable
