## 2. CLUSTER MUNITIONS DEEP DIVE — Your Must-Defend

**Facts [3](https://the-monitor.org/country-profile/brazil/cluster-munition-ban-policy?year=2023):**
- **Non-signatory** Brazil has not taken steps to join due to long-held objections to provisions + how convention negotiated.
- **Federal legislative proposal to ban rejected 14 Jan 2022.**
- **UNGA resolution promoting Convention:** Abstained since first introduced 2015; **absent Dec 2024 vote** (important — not even abstain, absent) [3](https://the-monitor.org/country-profile/brazil/cluster-munition-ban-policy?year=2023).
- **CCW history:** Supported efforts to create **new CCW protocol permitting use** of cluster munitions; put forward proposal; not proposed further CCW work since **2011 when effort failed** — effectively ended CCW deliberations, leaving Oslo as sole instrument [3](https://the-monitor.org/country-profile/brazil/cluster-munition-ban-policy?year=2023).
- **Justification offered:** Abstention due to objections over negotiation outside CCW + certain provisions [3](https://the-monitor.org/country-profile/brazil/cluster-munition-ban-policy?year=2023). OUP analysis adds: Oslo Article 21 interoperability + indefinite period mechanisms only small number advanced industries can meet [OUP].
- **Party to CCW Protocol V** on explosive remnants — regulates but not bans.

**OUP Analysis — Why Brazil banned APMs but not clusters (May 2025):** [2](https://academic.oup.com/fpa/article/21/3/oraf019/8136451)
- Initially **opposed APM ban**, favored regulation within **CCW Protocol II** (controls on transfers, moratorium on non-self-destruct/detectable). Based on concerns: (1) global power inequalities & Brazilian security, (2) format outside CCW + NGO participation.
- Change came when **Itamaraty + military** concluded banning APMs had **negligible security/economic costs** + **reputational benefits**. Former deputy FM Marcos Azambuja (1990-1992) gave three reasons: enhance Cardoso prestige, APMs cruel, no cost to security/industry [2](https://academic.oup.com/fpa/article/21/3/oraf019/8136451).
- May 1996: Voted FOR OAS APM resolution to avoid "alone" [2](https://academic.oup.com/fpa/article/21/3/oraf019/8136451). Same month: Itamaraty stressed risk of isolation if not joining Ottawa.
- **For clusters:** Cost-benefit different — adjustment costs higher (Avibras production), distribution concentrated, reputational pressure framed within government as not outweighing costs.

**Your DISEC defense script (30 sec):**
> "Brazil is party to all CCW Protocols including Protocol V, never used clusters, regulates under IHL. Our abstention was about strengthening inclusive CCW versus fragmented Oslo with Art.21 loophole. On LAWS, we apply same CCW-first logic *but* have learned delay costs — hence 42-state push before proliferation locks in, with verification standard we will apply to ourselves. That's consistency from 1996 landmine lesson: act before cost locks in, not after."

**One-liner:** "Regulate what we have under IHL, prohibit what can never comply — that's coherence, not cherry-picking."

## 3. MINE BAN — How Brazil Switched

**Timeline [2](https://academic.oup.com/fpa/article/21/3/oraf019/8136451):**
- Initially opposed ban, wanted CCW regulation (moratorium on exports to non-CCW, non-self-destruct).
- Voted FOR OAS resolution May 1996 to avoid being alone.
- Concluded no security/industry cost, prestige gain → joined Ottawa.

**Lesson for LAWS:** Use as example that Brazil *can* switch from "CCW regulation" to "ban" when window still open — and is doing so *early* for LAWS (2024) unlike 1996 delay.

## 4. ATT — Signed but Selling

**GA Vote 2 Apr 2013:** 154-3-23 adopted ATT [5](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/arms-trade-treaty-att/). Opened for signature 3 June 2013.

**Brazil:** Signed, ratified 2018 (National Congress delay due to prioritization, not opposition per Moraes 2019 [2](https://academic.oup.com/fpa/article/21/3/oraf019/8136451)).

**But:** Brazilian Report 19 Nov 2021 [4](https://brazilian.report/business/2021/11/19/selling-arms-conflict-zones-un-treaty/):
- "Brazil never signed Convention on Cluster Munitions and past/present governments have always abstained..."
- "Brazil is signatory of ATT, but data shows major weapons/munitions manufacturers still selling arms to war zones/places with poor human rights records — in astonishing quantities" — violating treaty spirit.

**Defense:** Acknowledge reporting, say Brazil supports ATT review mechanisms; for LAWS proposes *stronger* — mandatory certification + logging + assistance fund — to avoid ATT gaps. Forward-looking.
