# BRAZIL — 2026 CURRENT-STATUS AUDIT
> Purpose: audit the older Brazil research against the September 2026 evidence base before it is used for lobbying.

## 1. Verified core position

Brazil supports moving toward an international legally binding instrument on LAWS, with prohibitions and regulations and strong human control requirements. Brazil has repeatedly worked through the CCW GGE and has supported the rolling-text approach as a basis for negotiations.

A particularly important current diplomatic record is the Brazil-delivered joint statement on behalf of 42 States in 2025, later circulated into the CCW process, stating that the rolling text was sufficient as a basis to move toward negotiations.

## 3. Current 2026 context

The 2026 GGE met 2–6 March and 31 August–4 September 2026. The Chair's work continued to develop “elements of an instrument” through a rolling text.

The mandate is to formulate elements **by consensus without prejudging the legal nature** of the instrument.

Official:
https://meetings.unoda.org/meeting/79329
https://meetings.unoda.org/meeting/79329/documents

During the September session, the Chair circulated a revised draft final report. The report process included a set of elements and recommendations for the Seventh Review Conference concerning future work. This does **not** mean the CCW had already negotiated a treaty.

## 6. Claims that must be phrased carefully

### “MHC is a legal requirement”
Use:
> “Brazil argues that meaningful human control is necessary to comply with IHL principles…”

Do not present that sentence as a universally accepted proposition that all states already interpret identically.

### “IHL is not enough”
Use:
> “Brazil argues that existing IHL should be complemented by specific rules for autonomy…”

Do not say IHL does not apply.

### “IEEE 7007 is the international standard”
Incorrect.

Use:
> “Brazil has proposed IEEE STD 7007-2021 as a possible technical reference…”

It has not become a universally adopted LAWS verification standard.

### “The CCW consensus rule means one State has a veto”
More precise:
> “The relevant CCW GGE work is conducted by consensus, so unresolved disagreement can prevent agreement on collective outcomes.”

Do not transfer that procedural feature to UNGA voting.

### “166 States support a treaty”
Too broad.

Use:
> “166 States voted YES on A/RES/79/62.”

The resolution vote is not identical to an explicit endorsement of every possible treaty form.

### “42 States support Brazil's exact treaty”
Too broad.

Use:
> “42 States joined the 2025 joint statement supporting use of the rolling text as a basis for moving toward negotiations.”

## 8. Brazil's documented research vulnerabilities

These are research questions, not automatic “attacks”:

### Defence-industry contradiction
Brazil has a significant defence-industrial base and autonomous/AI-enabled research. Opponents may ask whether Brazil's proposed restrictions could constrain its own future capabilities.

### Human-control definition
Brazil must be able to define operationally what it means by meaningful human control, not merely repeat the phrase.

### Article 36 transparency
Brazil can be asked why existing national weapons-review obligations are insufficient and what additional international transparency it proposes.

### Legal-form sequencing
Brazil supports legally binding rules, but the CCW process itself has deliberately avoided prejudging the legal nature of the instrument. Brazil therefore needs to explain how it moves from the current GGE elements to negotiations without misrepresenting the procedural stage.

### Forum strategy
Brazil has historically valued the CCW but also supports broader UN involvement. The delegation should be prepared to explain sequencing and complementarity rather than presenting CCW and UNGA as mutually exclusive.
