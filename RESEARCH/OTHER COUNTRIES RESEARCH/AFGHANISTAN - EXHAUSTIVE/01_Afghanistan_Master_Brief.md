# AFGHANISTAN — LAWS COUNTRY DOSSIER
> Exact committee matrix | Brazil DISEC research | September 2026

## 1. Current position
No sufficiently specific current independent LAWS position was located in the present primary-source set. Afghanistan voted YES on A/RES/80/57. Afghanistan is a party to the CCW and has ratified Protocols I–III and later consented to Protocol IV; it also acceded to Additional Protocol I and II in 2024. These treaty commitments create a useful legal baseline, but they do not establish a present autonomous-weapons negotiating position.

## 2. Evidence status
Vote verified; current national LAWS position remains an evidence gap.

## 3. Negotiating strengths
Treaty participation in the conventional-weapons framework; ability to invoke humanitarian and IHL compliance.

## 4. Constraints / research vulnerabilities
Limited current independent public material located on LAWS; current representation and security conditions make it especially important not to infer a policy from group membership alone.

## 5. Brazil-specific research questions
Brazil should establish whether the delegation supports a binding instrument, a CCW-based approach, and what it means by human control before treating it as aligned on substance.

## 6. 2025 UNGA record
The official vote on A/RES/80/57 was recorded on 1 December 2025. The vote and sponsor information should be read together with the country's national position; a YES vote is not itself proof of support for every possible treaty mechanism.
