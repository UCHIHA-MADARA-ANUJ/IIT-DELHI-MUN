## 2. WEAPON PROGRAM (Hard Facts)

- **Definition trick:** China defines prohibited LAWS as needing ALL 5 traits cumulatively: (1) lethality + (2) full autonomy with no intervention possible DURING entire process + (3) impossibility of termination once started + (4) indiscriminate effects regardless of conditions/targets + (5) uncontrolled evolution. If ANY one is missing (e.g., you can press abort, or it distinguishes), it is NOT LAWS per China — so not banned. This is intentionally narrow [CJIP Mar 2023, Lieber 4 May 2026].
- **In practice:** Allows supervised "on the loop" autonomy (human watches, can intervene) rather than Brazil's "human approves each engagement." Protects swarms where one human supervises many drones.
- **Programs:** Mass swarm drones (Zhuhai demos 1000+), Wing Loong/Chengdu UAVs, AI-enabled command. Strategic ambiguity: supports ban rhetorically while developing capabilities. Analysts: "strategic ambiguity and optionality — protect industrial ability to flood market with autonomous hardware" [USANA 6 Jan 2026].
- **Why abstain not against:** Wants to be seen as responsible, not pariah like Russia; also wants to keep leverage over USA.

## 4. WEAKNESS — CONTRADICTION TO EXPLOIT

1.  **5-criteria bar is impossibly high:** If a weapon has a kill switch (so termination possible), China says not banned — even if it autonomously kills humans without distinction before switch. That's a loophole you can drive a swarm through.
2.  **Abstention vs rhetoric:** Says it supports binding instrument, but abstains on every resolution calling for it and blocks definition consensus — "when conditions are ripe" = never.
3.  **Human control vs industrial flood:** Wants to be seen as pro-human control while having industrial capacity to mass-produce swarms that make human control per engagement impossible.
4.  **On vs In the loop:** China's "on the loop" (supervisory) vs Brazil's "meaningful control per engagement" — supervisory fails when 100 drones attack at once (no time to intervene).
