# OTHER COUNTRIES RESEARCH — EXACT 86-MATRIX DOSSIERS

This folder is the **country-by-country research library** for the exact 86-country MUN matrix.

## Important scope rule

The repository contains research folders for some countries that are **not** in the user's 86-country committee matrix. Those folders are retained as background/reference material.

Use:
- `RESEARCH/08_Exact_86_Country_Research_Index.md` for the exact committee set.
- `RESEARCH/04_MUN_Country_Matrix_2026.md` for the current comparison table.

## Research standard for every matrix country

Each exact-matrix country must ultimately have a dossier covering:

### 1. Official position
- latest national statement;
- UNGA statements / explanations of vote;
- Secretary-General submission;
- CCW/GGE statements;
- working papers;
- group statements the country actually joined.

### 2. Voting and sponsorship history
- A/RES/78/241 (2023);
- A/RES/79/62 (2024);
- A/RES/80/57 (2025);
- 2025 A/C.1/80/L.41 sponsorship;
- relevant CCW consensus / process history.

### 3. Legal-form preference
- legally binding instrument;
- CCW protocol;
- political declaration;
- national measures / existing law;
- phased approach;
- conditions for negotiations.

### 4. Substantive position
- definition / scope;
- prohibited systems or uses;
- regulated systems or uses;
- human control / human judgment;
- IHL;
- Article 36 / weapons reviews;
- accountability / responsibility;
- verification / testing;
- lifecycle controls;
- geographic / temporal / target restrictions;
- transfer and proliferation;
- capacity-building.

### 5. Strategic and technological context
Only where evidenced:
- defence-industrial interests;
- AI/autonomy programmes;
- operational experience;
- technology dependence;
- relevant security environment.

These facts are **context**, not automatic proof of diplomatic policy.

### 6. Negotiating strengths
Documented assets such as:
- legal expertise;
- technical expertise;
- regional-group influence;
- CCW participation;
- drafting experience;
- affected-state perspective;
- cross-regional relationships.

### 7. Constraints / vulnerabilities
“Vulnerability” means a documented:
- policy inconsistency;
- unresolved position;
- legal-form disagreement;
- implementation problem;
- diplomatic constraint;
- capability/technology dependence;
- difference between national practice and stated policy.

Do not invent scandals or personal attacks.

### 8. Brazil-specific overlap and friction
Record:
- factual areas of overlap;
- factual areas of disagreement;
- questions Brazil should ask;
- concessions or clarifications that may matter later;
- evidence still missing.

This is **not** a bloc ranking.

### 9. Sources
Every major claim should point to:
1. primary UN / CCW / national source where available;
2. authoritative legal/humanitarian source if relevant;
3. secondary corroboration only where useful.

## Evidence labels

Use these labels inside dossiers:

- **VERIFIED — PRIMARY**
- **VERIFIED — SECONDARY**
- **HISTORICAL**
- **ANALYSIS / INFERENCE**
- **RESEARCH GAP**

A vote is never automatically equivalent to a full substantive position.

## Current research status

Some folders are already very detailed. Others still require a 2026 audit.

**“Existing dossier” does not mean “research complete.”**

Older material was produced at different times and with different evidence standards. Current preparation must be normalized to the 2026 source hierarchy.

## What we are deliberately NOT doing here yet

Do not lock:
- Brazil's final bloc;
- “enemy” / “ally” labels;
- MOD topics;
- GSLs;
- POIs;
- resolution clauses;
- lobbying scripts.

Those are later outputs of the research + live committee information, not assumptions to impose on the country dossiers.

## Workflow

**Exact country → primary evidence → current position → historical evolution → strengths/constraints → Brazil overlap → source audit → comparison.**
