# POSITION PAPER — BRAZIL | UNGA DISEC | LAWS
**Committee:** UNGA Disarmament & International Security Committee (DISEC) — First Committee
**Country:** Federative Republic of Brazil
**Agenda:** Establishing an International Regulatory Framework for the Development and Deployment of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS)
**Delegate:** IIT Delhi MUN — 26-27 September 2026

---

### 1. POSITION SUMMARY

Brazil supports a **legally binding instrument** ensuring **meaningful human control (MHC)** over the selection and engagement of targets by weapons systems. We advocate a **two-tier framework**:

- **Tier 1 — Prohibitions:** Ban on LAWS that (a) target humans directly, (b) cannot be understood/predicted/explained by the operator, (c) cannot be directed at a specific military objective, (d) cannot be limited in effects, or (e) operate without ability to abort/deactivate.
- **Tier 2 — Regulations:** For all other systems with autonomy in critical functions, positive obligations: human decision on target selection/engagement with situational awareness, strict geographic/temporal limits, supervision, intervention and deactivation capability, logging, and mandatory **Article 36** legal reviews.

Brazil's model is **CCW Protocol VI** based on the **GGE rolling text**, annexing **IEEE STD 7007-2021** as a common technical ontology. If CCW consensus continues to block, Brazil will support negotiations through UNGA, as **166 states voted for L.77/79/62 (Dec 2024)** and **164 for L.41/80/57 (Dec 2025)** demonstrate that consensus cannot equal veto.

### 2. NATIONAL CONTEXT & FOREIGN POLICY JUSTIFICATION

Brazil's Constitution (Art. 4) mandates prevalence of human rights, defense of peace, peaceful settlement of disputes, and multilateral cooperation. This — not alignment with any military bloc — drives our LAWS policy. Brazil seeks a permanent UNSC seat as a responsible norm-shaper; leadership on disarmament strengthens that claim. As a BRICS member differing from Russia/China on LAWS, Brazil demonstrates **principled autonomy**: we cooperate where possible, dissent where law requires.

Brazil has no LAWS programme and no doctrine requiring autonomy over human life. Our interest is **preventive**: stop an arms race that erodes IHL, lowers thresholds for force, and allows proliferation to non-state actors. Brazil's border surveillance system **SISFRON** uses autonomy for monitoring, not autonomous targeting — proof that autonomy is welcome **under human control**, not as replacement.

Domestically, **62% of Brazilians oppose killer robots** (2020 national poll, Campaign to Stop Killer Robots), giving democratic legitimacy to prohibition.

### 3. LEGAL BASIS — WHAT BRAZIL WILL CITE IN COMMITTEE

- **IHL Principles:** Distinction, Proportionality, Precaution, Military Necessity — require contextual human judgment; cannot be delegated to an algorithm in uncertain environments.
- **Martens Clause:** Civilians/combatants remain under protection of principles of humanity and dictates of public conscience.
- **Article 36, Additional Protocol I:** Obligation to review new weapons for IHL compliance. Brazil argues current national reviews are opaque and insufficient — we propose **mandatory, transparent reviews with common criteria**.
- **Geneva Conventions & CCW:** Brazil is party to CCW and all Protocols; we have shown CCW can host progressive law (we supported mine-ban through CCW before Ottawa, and note the cost of failure on cluster munitions where Brazil stayed outside the Oslo Convention precisely because it bypassed CCW without universality).
- **ICJ Advisory Opinion:** IHL applies to *all* weapons, past, present and future — LAWS are not in a legal vacuum.

### 5. BRAZIL'S 5 CORE ARGUMENTS (THE SPINE — MEMORIZE)

1. **MHC is law, not preference:** Without ability to understand/predict/explain and abort, a system cannot satisfy distinction/proportionality — therefore prohibition is legally required, not optional.
2. **Two-tier works for everyone:** Bans only the unacceptable (human-targeting, unpredictable, indiscriminate), regulates the rest with human control — protects innovation while preventing illegality. Already in rolling text 42 states accepted.
3. **Verifiability is solvable:** IEEE 7007-2021 + Article 36 + logging/certification makes MHC testable. "Cannot define LAWS" is no longer credible.
4. **Window is closing:** Kargu-2 (Libya 2021), Lavender/Gospel (Gaza), FPV swarms (Ukraine) show deployment without rules. Regulate before proliferation to non-state actors — Global South bears that cost first.
5. **Majority must not be vetoed:** 11 years of CCW GGE with consensus rule = zero binding outcome. One state blocking 166 is not multilateralism. If CCW fails, UNGA must act — as it did for mines, nuclear weapons.

### 6. WHAT BRAZIL PROPOSES — 6 OPERATIVE CLAUSES FOR YOUR RESOLUTION

1. **Establish CCW Protocol VI** on LAWS using the GGE rolling text as basis, with **Annex A: Tier 1 prohibitions** (list above, including prohibition of autonomous targeting of humans).
2. **Tier 2 Regulations:** Require human approval of each engagement with situational awareness, space/time limits, avoidance of civilian concentrations, supervision/intervention/deactivation, and mission-parameter lock without human validation.
3. **Technical Annex:** Adopt **IEEE STD 7007-2021** ontology for control mechanisms, with requirements for event logging, explainability, and pre-deployment certification.
4. **Legal Reviews:** Strengthen **Article 36** into **mandatory, transparent national reviews** with common reporting criteria to CCW/UNGA; create voluntary peer-review and capacity-building for developing states.
5. **Proliferation & Transfer Controls:** Prohibit transfer of Tier-1 systems; require end-use assurances and ATT-consistent transfer criteria for Tier-2; establish assistance for victims and for states affected by proliferation to NSAGs.
6. **Mandate & Timeline:** Mandate GGE to submit draft instrument to **7th CCW Review Conference (late 2026)** for negotiation; if consensus fails, recommend UNGA to establish open-ended working group; endorse **conclusion by end-2026** per SG/ICRC call.

Compromise room: Brazil can accept politically binding interim measures if they have **clear legal upgrade path** — but never accept "IHL is enough" or purely voluntary principles.

### 7. RED LINES & CONCESSIONS

**Never concede:** Prohibition of autonomous human targeting; requirement that MHC means ability to abort; that IHL alone is insufficient without specific instrument.

**Can flex:** Geographic scope of deactivation, exact logging retention period, whether IEEE annex is binding or recommendatory in year 1, capacity-building language for Global South, sequencing of CCW vs UNGA track.

Brazil will be attacked on: BRICS inconsistency (answer: principled autonomy), not joining Oslo Cluster Convention (answer: we favor universal CCW forum with loopholes fixed, same for LAWS via protocol), and "no LAWS program so no cost" (answer: precisely why we can be honest broker; SISFRON shows we use autonomy responsibly under control).
