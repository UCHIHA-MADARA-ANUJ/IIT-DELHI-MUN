# BRAZIL — CRISIS / CLOSING / INTERVENTION SPEECHES | DISEC LAWS

> Use only when triggered. Each under 60 sec unless noted. Keep Brazil's tone: law + numbers + solution.

---

## CRISIS UPDATE RESPONSE (if chair announces LAWS use in Gaza/Ukraine/Libya)

**60 sec:**
> "This crisis is not hypothetical. The panel report on Libya, the systems used in Gaza, and swarms deployed in Ukraine show what happens when law lags deployment. Under IHL, distinction and precaution require human judgment of context — no update to software fixes a weapon that cannot be aborted before it strikes. Brazil repeats its two-tier: prohibit autonomous human targeting and unpredictable systems now, regulate all others with human approval and verifiable deactivation. We ask the crisis author: will your system meet IEEE 7007 and Article 36 review, and can you guarantee it will not proliferate beyond this conflict? If not, it belongs in Tier 1."

**Why it wins:** You turn crisis into your framework, ask verifiable question, force answer.

## CLOSING SPEECH (90 sec — last GSL, after resolution)

> "In 11 years this file was studied. In one week this committee chose to act. Brazil came with a principle — no algorithm decides who lives — and a method — two tiers already endorsed by 166 states. This resolution prohibits what can never comply and regulates what can under human control, with IEEE standards and mandatory reviews so control is testable. It honors the Martens clause and the 42-state call to negotiate. The 6 who voted against LAWS regulation last December will call this premature. We call it preventive. Brazil asks the committee to adopt this text and send it to the Review Conference with a mandate, not a footnote. Let multilateralism mean majority with responsibility, not veto by expenditure."

**Close with:** Eye contact chair, nod to bloc, sit — don't over-thank.

## INTERVENTION BANK (20-30 sec, use when recognized on point of order / reply)

**1. When USA says "IHL is enough":**
> "Point of information: the US requires human judgment in Directive 3000.09 precisely because IHL requires it. Why oppose codifying its own standard in a treaty?"

**2. When Russia says "consensus protects sovereignty":**
> "Consensus is a method, not a veto. If one state can block 166, which sovereignty is protected?"

**3. When tech state says "cannot define LAWS":**
> "Definition is weapon system selecting and engaging without human intervention after activation — ICRC and Brazil's submission share it. If IEEE 7007 can standardize it, this committee can regulate it."

**4. When India/China say "strategic autonomy / development":**
> "Autonomy without rules is autonomy for non-state actors too. Our regulation tier protects innovation under human approval — does the delegate accept that or prefer no rules for anyone?"

**5. When asked about BRICS contradiction:**
> "BRICS is not a military alliance. Brazil exercises principled autonomy — we differ from partners on mines, on nuclear weapons, and today on LAWS, because law requires it."

**6. When asked about Cluster Munitions (Oslo) not joining:**
> "Brazil favored universal CCW with teeth over a treaty with interoperability loopholes. For LAWS we propose a CCW protocol — universal and binding — precisely to avoid that mistake."

**7. Chits — killer one-liners to send during debate:**
- To Chair (after your speech): "Brazil available to brief rolling text + 42-state statement — text attached"
- To Germany/France: "Two-tier: your regulation language + our prohibition list = joint OP1? — Brazil"
- To Nigeria/South Africa: "Proliferation to NSAGs — want co-sponsor transfer control clause? — Brazil"
