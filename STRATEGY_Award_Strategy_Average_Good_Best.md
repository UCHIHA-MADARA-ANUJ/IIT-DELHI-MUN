# AWARD STRATEGY — AVERAGE vs GOOD vs BEST DELEGATE | IIT DELHI MUN | DISEC LAWS | BRAZIL

> Brutal honesty: what actually gets you each award in Indian school/college MUN.

---

## AVERAGE DELEGATE (Participation/Verbal Mention) — What 60% do

- Reads BG, says "Brazil supports legally binding instrument"
- One GSL, one mod, generic: "LAWS are dangerous, need human control"
- No numbers, no treaties, no bloc. Waits for unmod, joins biggest group
- Chits: "Let's collaborate" with no clause
- Result: Chair notes you spoke, but no authorship. Forgettable.

**If you do only this, you will not be remembered.**

---

## GOOD DELEGATE (High Commendation / Special Mention) — What 15% do

- Cites 166 votes or 42-state statement once
- Mentions IHL, Article 36
- Active in unmods, co-sponsors resolution, maybe reads an OP
- Good chits, 2-3 POIs answered decently
- **Gap vs Best:** Speaks well but does not *frame* debate. Let USA or India set mod topics. Defends Brazil but doesn't attack contradiction. Resolution is group's, not theirs.

---

## BEST DELEGATE (What Brazil needs to do to win — 1 person/committee)

### 1. Research edge (visible in first 30 min)
- **First speech:** 166 votes + 42 states + IEEE 7007 + Article 36 in 90 sec. Chair immediately marks "researched."
- **Chit to dais after speech:** attach 42-state statement citation. Chair sees you know document, not BG.

### 2. Frame debate (propose mods)
- **Raise Motion 1 (Two-tier) yourself on Day 1 morning.** If it passes, entire committee debates on YOUR framework. That's authorship before resolution.
- **Raise Motion 3 (Technical standards) Day 1 afternoon** — shows depth no Latin bloc peer has. Chair notes range: law + tech + proliferation.

### 3. Isolate, don't brawl
- **Vs USA:** Don't shout "USA violates IHL." Ask: "Directive 3000.09 requires human judgment — why oppose treaty codifying it?" Makes USA defend contradiction, you stay diplomatic.
- **Vs Russia:** "GGE 11 years, one state blocks — what timeline does Russia propose, or is indefinite study the policy?" Polite, devastating.
- **Vs India (your real BD competitor in Indian MUN):** Flatter + trap: "India cites strategic autonomy — autonomy without rules means autonomy for NSAGs too. What verifiable standard does India propose?" Forces India to propose or look empty.

### 4. Own the paper (scribe tactic)
- **In first unmod, be the person typing.** Offer merged 6-OP text based on rolling text. Let Germany claim OP2, Nigeria OP5 — give credit, keep control. The scribe is de facto leader; sponsor list proves it.

### 5. POI dominance (3:1 ratio)
- **Ask 3 offensive POIs for every 1 you answer.** Prepare 15 offensive (USA×3, Russia×2, China×2, India×2, Israel×1, Turkey×1, UK×1, France×1) + 10 defensive with safe/strong/comeback variants.
- Example defensive: When hit on BRICS contradiction → safe: "Principled autonomy, BRICS not military alliance" → strong: "Same autonomy makes us credible broker" → comeback: "We differ on mines too — consistency is legal, not bloc loyalty."

### 6. Unmod activity (chair's main score sheet)
- Chairs in India score **number of chits + huddles + sponsors**. Send a chit every 15 min with **fact + ask**, not pleasantries. Have 20+ sponsors by Day 2 lunch.

### 7. Closing & voting
- Volunteer to present OPs. Defend Tier 1 bans against weakening amendments (whip bloc to vote NO on weakening). Closing speech ties 166 → 42 → your resolution → 2026 deadline.

**Best Delegate formula for Brazil:** *166 votes + 42-state statement + IEEE 7007 + two-tier in every speech + scribe in every unmod + 3:1 POI ratio + never compromise human-targeting ban.*

**The judge:** If you do average, you get participation. If you do good, you get High Comm. If you do above — propose mods, write paper, dominate POIs diplomatically, stay active — you take Best Delegate from India/USA who will otherwise out-shout you.
