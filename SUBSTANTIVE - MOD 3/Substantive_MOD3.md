# BRAZIL - SPEECH ARSENAL - DISEC LAWS
> Indian MUN style: tight, diplomatic, assertive. No cringe poetry. Every speech = hook + 1 fact + 1 solution + close.
> Time codes: GSL 60-90 sec (~130-150 words), Mod 40-45 sec (~90-110 words), Interventions 20-30 sec (~45-65 words)

---

## 1. OPENING SPEECH / GSL (90 seconds) - USE FIRST SPEECH

**Delivery note:** Stand, pause, eye contact with chair, then dias + blocs.

> "Brazil believes one principle decides this agenda: no algorithm should decide who lives and who dies.
>
> International humanitarian law already requires distinction, proportionality and precaution - principles that demand human judgment of context. Brazil's submission to the Secretary-General is clear: without meaningful human control - the ability to understand, predict, and interrupt a system before force is applied - a weapon cannot comply with IHL.
>
> That is why Brazil, with Ireland, Norway and Switzerland, led the joint statement of 42 states this March, calling the GGE rolling text a sufficient basis to start negotiations. That is why 166 states voted for a legally binding two-tier instrument last December - prohibitions for systems that can never comply, and strict regulations with human control for all others.
>
> Brazil proposes three concrete steps: a new CCW protocol based on the rolling text, mandatory Article 36 reviews anchored in IEEE Standard 7007-2021, and verifiable human supervision over every targeting decision within strict space and time limits.
>
> The window to regulate before deployment is closing - in Libya, Gaza and Ukraine we have seen what happens when it does. Brazil is ready to negotiate. The question is whether this committee will let one state veto 166."

**Word count:** ~148 | **Fact anchors:** 42-state statement, 166 votes, IEEE 7007, Article 36 | **Close:** Challenges blockers without naming

**Why it works in Indian MUN:** First line is memorable, not "honourable chair distinguished delegates we are extremely..." You state position + law + numbers + solution in 90 sec. Chair notes you know votes and standards, not just BG.

## 2. GSL VARIANT (60 seconds - if chair cuts time)

> "Brazil supports a legally binding instrument ensuring meaningful human control. IHL principles of distinction and proportionality already require it - a system that cannot be understood or aborted cannot be legal. On 2 December 2024, 166 states agreed this needs two tiers: ban autonomous targeting of humans and unpredictable systems, regulate the rest with human control, space-time limits and the ability to deactivate. Brazil co-sponsored that resolution and delivered the 42-state statement calling the rolling text ready for negotiations. We propose a CCW protocol with IEEE 7007 technical annex and mandatory Article 36 reviews. Consensus cannot be a veto when the threshold for war is being lowered."

## 3. MOD CAUCUS SPEECHES (Pick 4-5 to prepare; each 40-45 sec)

### MOD A: Human Control & IHL Compliance (MOST LIKELY MOD - RAISE IT YOURSELF)
*Motion:* "Deliberation on ensuring meaningful human control as prerequisite for IHL compliance of LAWS"

> "Brazil is clear: meaningful human control is not a policy option, it is the condition for legality. Control means the operator can understand the system, predict its effects, explain its targeting, and abort before force is applied. Without that, distinction and proportionality become software guesses in uncertain environments. Our 2024 submission notes this is implicit in IHL - especially the Martens clause. Therefore any instrument must prohibit systems that cannot meet this threshold, and require for all others human approval of each engagement with situational awareness and strict geographic and temporal limits."

**Points to push:** Define MHC operatively, cite Martens, propose prohibition language
**Allies to pull:** Austria, Chile, Holy See, Ireland

### MOD B: Two-Tier Framework (Prohibitions vs Regulations) (RAISE THIS - IT IS YOUR SPINE)
*Motion:* "Two-tier regulatory framework: prohibitions and regulations for LAWS"

> "Brazil supports the two-tier approach endorsed by the Secretary-General. Tier one: prohibit autonomous targeting of humans and any system that cannot be understood, directed at a military objective, or limited in effects. Tier two: regulate all other systems with positive obligations - human decision on critical functions, space-time limits, and verifiable deactivation. This is not theory; it is the rolling text 42 states already called ready for negotiation, and it is what 166 states voted for. It lets us ban the unacceptable while keeping space for responsible innovation under human control."

**Points:** Concrete ban list + regulation list, cites rolling text
**Allies:** Germany, France, Netherlands (they like tiered)

### MOD C: Legal Reviews & Technical Standards (YOUR UNIQUE VALUE - BRAZIL OWNS IEEE 7007)
*Motion:* "Role of Article 36 reviews and technical standards in regulating LAWS"

> "Verifiability answers the objection that LAWS cannot be defined. Article 36 of Additional Protocol I already obliges legal review of new weapons, but reviews are national and opaque. Brazil's Working Paper 1 of 2024 proposed IEEE Standard 7007-2021 as a common ontological framework - shared language for control mechanisms, target limits, duration and geography. Coupled with mandatory logging and certification, this makes human control testable, not rhetorical. A new protocol should annex this standard and require states to publish review criteria, reducing legal risk for all."

**Points:** IEEE 7007, Article 36, logging/certification
**Allies:** Japan, South Korea, Canada (technical states)

### MOD D: Arms Race, Proliferation & Global South Security
*Motion:* "Impact of LAWS on international peace, arms races and proliferation"

> "LAWS lower the threshold for force, accelerate escalation, and proliferate beyond state control. Unlike nuclear weapons, autonomy is software - cheap and dual-use. Brazil's border surveillance program, SISFRON, shows why norms matter: we use human-in-the-loop systems for the Amazon, under legal review, precisely to avoid an autonomy race in our region. Without pre-emptive rules, non-state actors will acquire capabilities major powers normalize today. That is why the 2026 deadline from the Secretary-General matters - if we wait until deployment is complete, regulation becomes impossible."

**Points:** Threshold lowering, proliferation to NSAs, SISFRON example (pre-empts attack)
**Allies:** South Africa, Nigeria, Sierra Leone, NAM

### MOD E: CCW vs UNGA - Forum & Process
*Motion:* "Way forward: role of CCW GGE rolling text and UNGA informal consultations"

> "Brazil presided over the GGE in 2022-2023; we value the CCW. Yet as Brazil stated in 2024, inclusivity cannot be an excuse for delay. Eleven years of GGE have produced no mandate because consensus allows one state to block 124. Brazil therefore supports the rolling text as basis for negotiation within the CCW, while welcoming UNGA informal consultations as complementary - as we voted for in Explanation of Vote 2023. We should agree here to recommend the Seventh Review Conference authorize negotiations, aiming to conclude by end 2026."

**Points:** Defend CCW but call out veto abuse, dual track
**Allies:** Ireland, Norway, Switzerland (co-drafters)

### MOD F: Ethical & Human Dignity Dimensions (If human rights mod arises)
*Motion:* "Ethical perspectives and human dignity in deployment of LAWS"

> "Beyond law, Brazil echoes our 2021 GGE statement: delegating kill decisions to algorithms violates human dignity. A machine cannot bear responsibility; without accountability, there is no compliance. That gap is most dangerous for civilians and for the right to life. That is why prohibitions must coverAWS that target humans, and regulations must keep humans accountable through a clear chain of command."

## 4. INTERVENTIONS (20-30 sec) - USE WHEN YOU CAN'T GET GSL

**A. When USA says "IHL is enough":**
> "If IHL alone is enough, why does Article 36 require every state to review new weapons? And if your own Ethical AI Principles demand human judgment, why oppose writing that into law? Brazil asks the US to explain the gap."

**B. When Russia says "CCW only":**
> "Brazil respects the CCW, but 166 states voting for complementary UNGA talks while Russia voted with 2 others suggests the majority see CCW consensus as a block. Does Russia believe one state should veto 166?"

**C. To pull fence-sitters:**
> "Germany and France want regulation, not just ban. Brazil's two-tier offers exactly that - ban targeting humans, regulate the rest with space-time limits. Can we agree on that tier?"

**D. Quick stat drop:**
> "62 percent of Brazilians, 120 states, 42 co-signatories, 166 votes. This is not activism - this is the majority. Brazil asks the committee to move from discussion to negotiation."

## 5. CLOSING SPEECH (60 sec - END OF COMMITTEE)

> "When Brazil began this debate, we said no algorithm should decide life and death. This committee has now shown whether it means to prevent that or to excuse it.
>
> The majority has been clear: prohibit autonomous targeting of humans, require meaningful human control with space, time and deactivation limits, anchor reviews in IEEE 7007, and negotiate the rolling text into a protocol before the 2026 deadline.
>
> Brazil did not ask for perfection. We asked for a legally binding line the world can verify.
>
> The costs of delay are not abstract - in Gaza, Libya, Ukraine, autonomy is already being tested on human lives. The costs of action are modest - a protocol, a standard, a review.
>
> Those who block will have to explain to 166 states why one veto outweighed 85 percent of the world. Brazil will not sign that blank cheque. We hope this committee will not either."

## DELIVERY TIPS FOR INDIAN MUN

- **Don't read.** Memorize structure: Hook (7 words) + Law (MHC) + Fact (166/42/IEEE) + Solution (protocol + reviews) + Challenge (window closing/ veto).
- **Pause after "no algorithm should decide who lives and who dies."** Let it land.
- **Say numbers slowly:** "One - six - six states" not "one-sixty-six" rushed.
- **Use "Brazil believes / Brazil proposes" not "I think".**
- **Yield to POIs from allies, not from blockers if question is hostile and you are short on time.** In Indian MUN, chair respects "Yield to chair" when you have made your point.
