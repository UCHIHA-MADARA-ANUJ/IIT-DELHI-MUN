# India — Brazil GSL POI Arsenal

## GSL only

### 1. Strongest
> **“India voted in favour of Resolution 80/57, which calls for work towards completing elements for an instrument with a view to future negotiation, yet India does not support negotiating a legally binding LAWS instrument. What binding outcome does India believe this process should ultimately produce?”**

**Simplified:**
> “India voted for the resolution moving toward an instrument, but opposes a binding treaty. So what binding result does India actually want?”

### 2. Existing IHL / new rules
> **“India has argued that the CCW GGE should continue addressing the issue comprehensively rather than moving prematurely to a legally binding instrument. What specific gap in existing international law does India believe must be solved before it would support negotiations?”**

**Simplified:**
> “What exact legal problem does India want solved before it supports a treaty?”

### 3. Human control
> **“If India agrees that human responsibility and compliance with international humanitarian law must remain central to the use of force, what prevents those requirements from being translated into binding international standards for autonomous weapons?”**

**Simplified:**
> “If India supports human responsibility, why not make the rules for it legally binding?”

### 4. Predictability
> **“If India does not support a binding deployment framework, what common international threshold should determine whether an autonomous system is sufficiently predictable and controllable for use in a civilian-populated environment?”**

**Simplified:**
> “Without binding rules, who decides when a LAWS is predictable enough to deploy?”

### 5. Best follow-up
> **“If India believes further discussion is necessary, what concrete safeguard does India propose adopting now rather than waiting for consensus on a future framework?”**

**Simplified:**
> “What safeguard does India want now, instead of waiting?”

## Factual basis
India does not support negotiation of a legally binding LAWS instrument. It voted in favour of UNGA Resolution 80/57 in December 2025, while its earlier explanation of vote emphasized the ongoing CCW GGE process. India has described binding proposals as premature pending further exploration of options.
