# Israel — Brazil GSL POI Arsenal

## GSL only

### 1. Strongest
> **“Israel maintains that existing international humanitarian law is sufficient to regulate autonomous weapons. If that is sufficient, what LAWS-specific international obligation does Israel support to ensure that human control, testing and operational limits are actually implemented before deployment?”**

**Simplified:**
> “If existing IHL is enough, what new LAWS-specific rule does Israel support to make sure these weapons are actually controlled?”

### 2. Binding instrument
> **“If Israel opposes a legally binding LAWS instrument, how does it propose creating a common international minimum standard when States can interpret and implement existing obligations differently?”**

**Simplified:**
> “If there is no binding LAWS treaty, how do we get one common international standard?”

### 3. Operational control
> **“If existing IHL already governs autonomous weapons, does Israel accept that a system's target categories, geographical scope, duration and operational conditions must be constrained before deployment and throughout its operation?”**

**Simplified:**
> “Does Israel accept binding limits on where, when and against whom an autonomous weapon can operate?”

### 4. Accountability
> **“If an autonomous system produces an unlawful effect despite being authorized under existing IHL procedures, what additional mechanism does Israel support for determining responsibility for the system's autonomous behaviour?”**

**Simplified:**
> “If an autonomous weapon causes an unlawful result, what mechanism decides who is responsible?”

### 5. Best follow-up
> **“If Israel's answer is that existing IHL already provides that protection, why oppose a treaty that would make the relevant safeguards specific, transparent and internationally enforceable?”**

**Simplified:**
> “If IHL already gives the protection, why oppose making the safeguards clearer and binding?”

## Factual basis
Israel does not support negotiating a legally binding instrument. It has stated that existing IHL is sufficient to regulate future weapons with autonomous capabilities. It voted against UNGA Resolution 80/57 in December 2025. Current monitoring records this position as of June 2026.
