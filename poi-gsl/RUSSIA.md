# Russia — Brazil GSL POI Arsenal

## Agenda
**Establishing an International Regulatory Framework for the Development and Deployment of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS)**

## Why Russia is a target
Russia voted against UNGA Resolution 80/57 on LAWS in December 2025. At the CCW, Russia has stated that human control is necessary, but that the **specific forms and methods of human control should be left to State discretion**, and that effective control can exist beyond direct control. Russia's own 2022 AI-weapons concept also says humans remain responsible while leaving the specific forms and methods of control to the State. citeturn0search0turn1search14

The strongest Brazilian attack is therefore not “Russia has no safeguards.” It is:

**If Russia accepts human control and operational safeguards, why should the minimum level of that control be left entirely to each State when the weapon itself can make lethal decisions?**

---

# 1. BEST POI — STATE DISCRETION

> **“Russia states that the specific forms and methods of human control should remain at the discretion of each State. If two States deploy the same autonomous targeting capability under two completely different standards of human control, which international minimum does Russia believe protects civilians?”**

### Simplified
> **“If every country decides its own level of human control, what minimum protects civilians everywhere?”**

### Why it hits
This goes directly at Russia's documented position that the form and method of control should remain State discretion. It also stays entirely within the LAWS agenda.

---

# 2. BEST IF RUSSIA SAYS “IHL ALREADY APPLIES”

> **“Russia maintains that existing international law, including IHL, applies to AI-enabled weapons. If that is sufficient, what prevents States from interpreting the level of human control required for distinction, proportionality and precautions differently when deploying autonomous systems?”**

### Simplified
> **“If IHL is enough, who decides how much human control is required?”**

### Follow-up
> **“And if each State decides that for itself, where is the common international threshold?”**

---

# 3. REAL-WORLD RUSSIAN POLICY VS INTERNATIONAL MINIMUM

> **“Russia's own AI-weapons concept requires human responsibility and human control, while leaving the method of that control to national discretion. Why should a safeguard Russia considers necessary be defined by the State using the weapon rather than by a common international minimum?”**

### Simplified
> **“If Russia says human control is necessary, why should each country decide what counts as enough control?”**

---

# 4. PREDICTABILITY / DEPLOYMENT POI

> **“Russia says effective human control can exist beyond direct control. What minimum level of predictability must a State demonstrate before Russia considers indirect control sufficient to authorize autonomous target identification, selection or engagement?”**

### Simplified
> **“If humans don't directly control the system, how predictable must it be before Russia considers that safe enough to deploy?”**

### Why it hits
This connects Russia's own “control beyond direct control” formulation to the central deployment question: **predictability before lethal autonomy is authorized.**

---

# 5. THE OPERATIONAL-LIMITS QUESTION

> **“Russia has identified safeguards such as limits on target types, duration, geographical scope, testing and the possibility of intervention. Does Russia support making those safeguards common international minimums, or should every State decide its own threshold?”**

### Simplified
> **“Should all countries have the same minimum safeguards, or can every country set its own?”**

### Follow-up
If they say “context matters”:

> **“Brazil agrees that context matters. What binding minimum remains regardless of context?”**

---

# 6. THE CHANGING-CONDITIONS POI

> **“If Russia accepts that human control depends on the operational context, what happens when that context materially changes after authorization? Does Russia support mandatory reassessment before autonomous force continues?”**

### Simplified
> **“If the battlefield changes after approval, must the weapon be reassessed before it keeps operating?”**

### Follow-up
> **“If yes, why should that reassessment not be an international requirement?”**

---

# 7. THE RESPONSIBILITY TRAP

> **“Russia correctly states that humans remain responsible throughout the life cycle of AI-enabled weapons. But if the State chooses the level of human control, what prevents responsibility from becoming purely formal when the system produces an effect that its operator could not reasonably anticipate?”**

### Simplified
> **“If humans are responsible, how do they remain responsible for something they could not reasonably predict?”**

---

# 8. SHORT, HARD POI

> **“If human control is necessary, why should the State deploying the weapon be allowed to define for itself what ‘control’ means?”**

### Simplified
> **“If control is necessary, why should each country define it differently?”**

## Factual basis
Russia's 2023 CCW working paper, based on its 2022 Concept of Activities of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation in the Development and Use of Weapons Systems with AI Technologies, states that humans should retain responsibility and that human control should be maintained, while the specific forms and methods of that control should be left to State discretion; it also says effective human control can be achieved beyond direct control. citeturn1search14

Russia voted against UNGA Resolution 80/57, adopted 164-6-7 on 1 December 2025. citeturn0search0

The 2025 CCW discussions specifically addressed context-appropriate human judgment and control, including the need for systems to act foreseeably and for human judgement/control to be sufficient for compliance with IHL. citeturn1search12turn1search13
