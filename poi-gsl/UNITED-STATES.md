# United States — Brazil GSL POI Arsenal

## Agenda
**Establishing an International Regulatory Framework for the Development and Deployment of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS)**

## Use these for GSL only
These are designed for a delegate's **GSL speech**, so they attack broad policy claims the delegate is likely to make rather than turning the POI into a technical MOD-1 question.

---

# 1. BEST POI — U.S. SAFEGUARDS VS INTERNATIONAL RULES

> **“The United States already requires its autonomous weapons to undergo rigorous testing, operate within defined geographic and temporal limits, and obtain additional human input when those parameters cannot be maintained. If Washington recognises these safeguards as necessary for its own forces, why should Brazil accept anything less as an international legal obligation?”**

### Simplified
> **“America already requires these safeguards for its own weapons. Why shouldn't they be legally required for every State?”**

### Why this is strong
This does **not** falsely claim the U.S. has no safeguards. It uses the U.S.'s own weapons policy to ask why safeguards it considers necessary domestically should not become international minimum requirements.

---

# 2. BEST IF THEY SAY “EXISTING IHL IS ENOUGH”

> **“The United States argues that existing international humanitarian law is sufficient to govern autonomous weapons. Yet the United States itself imposes additional safeguards specifically for autonomous weapons. If IHL alone is sufficient, why does Washington need these additional rules in the first place?”**

### Simplified
> **“If existing IHL is enough, why does America itself need extra rules for autonomous weapons?”**

### Follow-up
> **“Then why should those additional safeguards remain national policy rather than becoming international minimum requirements?”**

---

# 3. BEST IF THEY TALK ABOUT “APPROPRIATE HUMAN JUDGMENT”

> **“The United States calls for ‘appropriate levels of human judgment’ over the use of force. But if the standard is left to each State to determine, what prevents one State from calling minimal supervision ‘appropriate’ while another requires meaningful intervention?”**

### Simplified
> **“If every country decides what ‘appropriate’ human judgment means, what stops countries from setting completely different standards?”**

### Why it works
It attacks the **ambiguity**, not the existence of human judgment. That is much harder to dismiss as misrepresenting the U.S. position.

---

# 4. BEST IF THEY CLAIM THEIR NATIONAL SAFEGUARDS ARE SUFFICIENT

> **“The United States already accepts safeguards concerning testing, target restrictions, operational limits and human input. Which of these safeguards does Washington believe should *not* become a minimum international requirement for States deploying autonomous weapons?”**

### Simplified
> **“Which safeguard does America think should NOT be internationally required?”**

### Follow-up if they say “all of them”
> **“Then what prevents the same safeguards from being codified in a binding international instrument?”**

---

# 5. BEST IF THEY CLAIM HUMAN RESPONSIBILITY IS ENOUGH

> **“The United States maintains that humans remain responsible for the use of force. But when an autonomous system operates beyond the conditions actually assessed by the human decision-maker, what concrete international obligation requires that decision to be reassessed?”**

### Simplified
> **“If the system goes beyond what the human assessed, what binding rule requires the human to reassess it?”**

---

# 6. STRONG REAL-WORLD POLICY ATTACK

> **“U.S. policy requires autonomous systems to demonstrate performance and reliability under realistic conditions and to remain within intended operational parameters. If Washington accepts that predictability must be demonstrated before deployment, what common international threshold does it support for deciding when a LAWS is predictable enough to deploy?”**

### Simplified
> **“If predictability must be proven before deployment, what international standard decides when a LAWS is predictable enough?”**

---

# 7. THE INTERNATIONAL-DOUBLE-STANDARD QUESTION

> **“If the United States considers rigorous testing, operational limits and human safeguards necessary before its own autonomous weapons are deployed, why should civilians in a conflict involving another State receive protection that depends only on that State's voluntary standards?”**

### Simplified
> **“Why should civilian protection depend on each country's voluntary rules?”**

---

# 8. VERY SHORT / HARD-HITTING

> **“If America accepts these safeguards for its own autonomous weapons, what exactly prevents it from accepting the same safeguards as an international legal minimum?”**

### Simplified
> **“If you already accept the safeguards, why not make them international law?”**

---

# QUICK CHOICE GUIDE

### If their GSL says:
**“America already has strict safeguards.”**
→ Use **#1 or #4**.

**“Existing IHL is enough.”**
→ Use **#2**.

**“We support appropriate human judgment.”**
→ Use **#3**.

**“Humans remain responsible.”**
→ Use **#5**.

**“Our weapons are tested and reliable.”**
→ Use **#6**.

**“National implementation is enough.”**
→ Use **#7**.

### If you only get one chance
**Use #1.**

It is grounded in actual U.S. weapons policy and forces the delegate to confront the exact Brazilian proposal: **why should safeguards that the U.S. itself considers necessary remain merely national rather than becoming international minimum requirements?**

## Factual basis
U.S. Department of Defense Directive 3000.09 requires autonomous weapon systems to undergo rigorous verification and validation, operate within intended time/geographic parameters, and include measures for termination or additional operator input when required conditions cannot be maintained. U.S. policy also uses the formulation “appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force.”

The POIs above deliberately do **not** claim that the U.S. lacks regulation, rejects IHL, or rejects human responsibility. The pressure point is the gap between **national safeguards** and **binding international minimum standards**.
