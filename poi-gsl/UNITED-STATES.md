# POI — United States

## Strategic target
The U.S. is a high-value target because it supports IHL compliance and appropriate levels of human judgment, but opposes making "human control" a standalone international legal standard and has argued that a legally binding instrument is not necessary. In the 2025 UNGA vote on Resolution 80/57, the U.S. voted against the resolution (164–6–7 overall).

## Best POI
> **The United States calls for “appropriate levels of human judgment”; who defines what is “appropriate” — an internationally binding standard, or the State deploying the weapon?**

## Stronger POI — if they defend national discretion
> **If the level of human judgment is determined by each State, what prevents two States from declaring completely different levels of human involvement “appropriate” for the same autonomous function?**

## Binding-law POI
> **If existing international humanitarian law is sufficient, what additional binding mechanism does the United States propose to ensure that autonomous systems remain within their lawful operating parameters after deployment?**

## Human-control POI
> **If a human can technically intervene but cannot meaningfully understand or predict the system's behaviour in time, does the United States consider that genuine human control?**

## Predictability POI — useful for MOD 1
> **If predictability is context-dependent, what minimum international threshold does the United States support before an autonomous system may be deployed in a new operational environment?**

## Operational-change POI
> **If a system's mission parameters or operational conditions materially change after authorization, does the United States support mandatory reassessment before continued autonomous operation?**

## Killer short POI
> **Who defines “appropriate” — international law, or the State using the weapon?**

## Follow-up if they say “IHL already applies”
> **Then what prevents a State from claiming its own safeguards were sufficient after an autonomous system causes an unlawful effect?**

## Follow-up if they say “human judgment is enough”
> **Judgment over what — the decision to deploy the system, or the system's actual target identification and engagement during operation?**

## Follow-up if they say “existing law is enforceable”
> **What specific international verification mechanism would you support to verify compliance during operation rather than only after harm occurs?**

## Evidence / research notes
- UNGA Resolution 80/57 was adopted on 1 December 2025 by 164 votes in favour, 6 against and 7 abstentions. The United States voted against.
- U.S. policy has favored the formulation “appropriate levels of human judgment” rather than treating “human control” as a standalone legal standard.
- A U.S. State Department advisory report stated that the U.S. considers existing IHL strong and does not view a legally binding LAWS instrument as necessary.
- CCW discussions distinguish “context-appropriate human judgement and control” from a universal requirement of direct human oversight; the key dispute is how that concept should be operationalized and whether it should become binding.

## Brazil's angle
Do not accuse the U.S. of rejecting human responsibility or IHL. The stronger attack is narrower: Brazil supports a legally binding two-tier framework and wants operational requirements that make human control concrete rather than leaving “appropriate” undefined. Force the U.S. to specify the international standard, verification mechanism, and response when system behaviour or operating conditions materially change.
