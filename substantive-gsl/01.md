# 01 — Accountability Gap

## Brazil — Substantive GSL Chit

### Core issue
**Autonomy cannot create a legal vacuum between the person who deploys a weapon and the consequences it produces.**

Brazil's LAWS position goes beyond simply saying that a human should be somewhere in the chain of command. The deployment of an autonomous weapon must preserve the ability to attribute legal responsibility for its use and effects.

### Brazil's substantive position
A LAWS configuration should not be considered permissible merely because a human authorized its deployment beforehand.

Before deployment, the State must be able to establish a credible chain of responsibility for:

- who authorizes the use of the system;
- who defines and approves the operational context;
- who validates the system's critical functions and limitations;
- who is responsible for monitoring and intervention where required; and
- who bears legal responsibility if the system produces an unlawful effect.

If the autonomous nature of the system makes responsibility impossible to meaningfully attribute, that is not a reason to dilute accountability. It is a reason to question whether that configuration should be deployed at all.

### The distinction to remember
**Human involvement ≠ human control ≠ accountability.**

A person pressing “deploy” does not automatically mean that every subsequent autonomous consequence can be attributed to that person.

The real test is whether the human and State responsibility chain remains legally meaningful throughout the authorized use of force.

### Killer line
> **“Autonomy cannot become a legal vacuum between the person who deploys the weapon and the consequences it produces.”**

### If challenged
**Delegate:** “The commander is responsible.”

**Brazil:** “Then what exactly is the commander legally responsible for if the system's subsequent behaviour was neither sufficiently understood nor attributable to a decision the commander actually made?”

**Delegate:** “Existing IHL already establishes responsibility.”

**Brazil:** “Then the instrument should ensure that an autonomous system cannot be deployed in circumstances where existing responsibility rules become practically impossible to apply.”

### Evidence anchor
Brazil has argued in the CCW process that autonomous weapon systems must remain compatible with international humanitarian law and that the use of such systems must not preclude attribution of State or individual legal responsibility. This gives Brazil a substantive accountability argument distinct from the predictability, operational-limits, and two-tier-framework material already used in its GSL.

### Do NOT repeat in the GSL
The current Brazil GSL already covers:
- Lavender / “Where's Daddy?” and Kargu-2;
- U.S. and Russian positions;
- pre-deployment review vs operational constraints;
- target categories, geography, duration and mission parameters;
- reassessment after material changes;
- Brazil's two-tier instrument;
- Resolution 80/57 and the 164-vote figure.

Use this chit as **new substantive ammunition** in POIs, caucus, or responses when another delegate claims that prior human authorization automatically solves the accountability problem.
