# SUBSTANTIVE GSL CHIT 01
## BRAZIL — ACCOUNTABILITY & ATTRIBUTION

**Committee:** CCW / GGE on LAWS  
**Country:** Federative Republic of Brazil  
**Agenda:** Establishing thresholds for the deployment of LAWS  
**Use:** GSL / POIs / Moderated Caucus / Rebuttals

---

## 01. THE CORE ISSUE

### ACCOUNTABILITY CANNOT BE DELEGATED TO A MACHINE.

The central question is not simply whether a human was involved before an autonomous weapon was deployed.

The real question is:

> **Can legal responsibility still be meaningfully attributed to a State or individual for the consequences produced by the system?**

Brazil's position is that an autonomous weapons system should not be considered compatible with IHL if its use may **preclude attribution of State or individual legal responsibility**.

---

## 02. BRAZIL'S POSITION

Brazil does **not** accept the argument that:

> “A human authorized the weapon, therefore accountability is solved.”

Authorization alone is insufficient.

For deployment to remain legally defensible, the responsibility chain must remain meaningful throughout the use of force.

Before deployment, the responsible authorities must be able to establish:

- **WHO** authorizes the system;
- **WHO** determines the operational context;
- **WHO** validates the system's critical functions;
- **WHO** is responsible for monitoring and intervention;
- **WHO** remains legally responsible for the consequences of its use.

If autonomous operation makes those questions practically impossible to answer, the problem is not that accountability needs to be weakened.

**The problem is that the system's use may not be legally permissible in that configuration.**

---

## 03. THE BRAZILIAN TEST

### Ask three questions:

**1. Can the system be meaningfully controlled?**  
If not → IHL compliance is compromised.

**2. Can its relevant effects be sufficiently understood, predicted and explained?**  
If not → deployment becomes legally problematic.

**3. Can responsibility for its use and consequences still be attributed to a State or individual?**  
If not → Brazil considers that a fundamental accountability problem.

### Therefore:

> **No meaningful control + no sufficient understanding/predictability + no meaningful attribution = no defensible deployment.**

---

## 04. WHY THIS MATTERS

A conventional weapon does not make the legal decision itself.

With an autonomous weapon, the relationship between:

**human decision → machine behaviour → physical effect**

can become significantly more complex.

That creates a dangerous possibility:

**Commander:** “I authorized the system.”  
**Operator:** “I was only supervising it.”  
**Developer:** “I did not determine the target.”  
**Manufacturer:** “The system operated as designed.”  
**Machine:** *cannot bear legal responsibility.*

### Brazil's position:

**That chain cannot end with nobody meaningfully accountable.**

Autonomy must never become an accountability gap.

---

## 05. WHAT BRAZIL WANTS IN THE INSTRUMENT

A future legally binding instrument should ensure that:

### BEFORE DEPLOYMENT
- responsibility for the system's use is identifiable;
- critical functions are subject to meaningful human control;
- the system's relevant behaviour can be sufficiently understood and assessed;
- operational conditions are defined;
- the responsible chain of command is established.

### DURING DEPLOYMENT
- human responsibility remains active;
- the system remains within its authorized operational conditions;
- intervention remains possible where required;
- critical changes cannot escape human validation.

### IF ACCOUNTABILITY CANNOT BE MAINTAINED
**The system should not be deployed in that configuration.**

---

## 06. KILLER LINES

> **“The machine may execute force; it cannot inherit responsibility for that force.”**

> **“Autonomy cannot become a legal vacuum between the person who deploys the weapon and the consequences it produces.”**

> **“A human signature on an authorization form cannot become a substitute for meaningful legal accountability.”**

> **“If nobody can clearly answer who remains responsible when the machine acts, the system has already exposed the weakness in the legal framework.”**

---

## 07. IF A DELEGATE SAYS...

### “The commander remains responsible.”

**Brazil:**
> “Then the framework must ensure that the commander can actually be held responsible for the autonomous effects produced under that authorization. How is that responsibility preserved when the system's behaviour cannot be sufficiently understood or attributed?”

### “Existing IHL already establishes responsibility.”

**Brazil:**
> “Brazil agrees that existing IHL applies. The question is whether the use of an autonomous system can practically prevent those existing rules from being applied. Our instrument must close that accountability gap rather than assume it does not exist.”

### “Humans are always involved.”

**Brazil:**
> “Human involvement is not the same as meaningful control or accountability. The question is whether the human retains the ability and responsibility to govern the critical use of force.”

### “The system is only a tool.”

**Brazil:**
> “A tool does not remove the State or individual responsibility for its use. Precisely for that reason, the legal framework must ensure that autonomous operation does not make attribution practically impossible.”

---

## 08. POI-READY VERSION

### Main POI
> **“If a State cannot clearly attribute responsibility for an autonomous weapon's unlawful effects to a responsible human decision-maker, on what legal basis should that system have been deployed?”**

### Simplified
> **“If nobody can clearly be held responsible when the system causes an unlawful effect, why was it deployed?”**

---

## 09. BRAZILIAN SOURCE BASIS

Brazil's 2024 submission on autonomous weapon systems states that LAWS raise fundamental challenges concerning **meaningful human control and accountability**. It further states that an AWS would be incompatible with IHL if its use may **preclude attribution of State and individual legal responsibilities for the consequences of its use**. It also links meaningful human control to the ability to understand, predict and explain relevant effects. 

Brazil therefore has a direct substantive basis for treating **accountability and attribution as a deployment condition**, rather than merely as an issue to consider after an incident.

**Primary source:** Brazil's submission to the UN on autonomous weapon systems, 2024.
